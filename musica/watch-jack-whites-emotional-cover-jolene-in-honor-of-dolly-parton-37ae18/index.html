Watch Jack White’s Emotional Cover ‘Jolene’ in Honor of Dolly Parton

What's happening

Jack White performed an emotional cover of 'Jolene' during a London concert as a tribute to Dolly Parton following her death. The rock musician's rendition honored the country legend and her enduring musical legacy.

Who's involved

Jack White, the guitarist and lead vocalist of the White Stripes who built a successful solo career after the band's 2011 split, performed the tribute. Dolly Parton, the 'Queen of Country' and one of the most honored country performers in history with eleven Grammy Awards and numerous other accolades, passed away on Tuesday.

Why it matters

The performance underscores the cross-genre influence Parton wielded throughout her career, with major rock and pop artists recognizing her songwriting genius. White's public tribute during a live concert demonstrates how Parton's work transcended country music and shaped artists across musical boundaries, cementing her legacy beyond her genre.

The story

Jack White took to the stage in London to deliver a poignant cover of 'Jolene,' Dolly Parton's most iconic song, in honor of the country music legend who died earlier this week. According to Rolling Stone, the musician performed the emotional rendition during his concert as a tribute to Parton following her passing on Tuesday. The performance marked a significant public acknowledgment of Parton's influence on musicians working across genres.

Dolly Parton, who earned the title 'Queen of Country,' was one of the most honored country performers in history. Over her legendary career, she accumulated eleven Grammy Awards, three Emmy Awards, the Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award, and nominations for two Academy Awards, six Golden Globe Awards, and a Tony Award. Beyond her musical achievements, Parton became a cultural icon whose compositions and recordings touched audiences far beyond country music's traditional boundaries.

'Jolene,' one of Parton's most recognizable songs, became a standard covered by artists across multiple genres. The song's emotional vulnerability and universal themes made it a vehicle for tributes from performers who may not have recorded country music themselves. White's decision to perform the song in concert demonstrated how deeply Parton's work had penetrated rock and alternative music circles.

Jack White, who was the guitarist and lead vocalist of the rock duo the White Stripes, became a key figure in the 2000s indie and garage rock movements before launching a solo career following the band's 2011 split. His willingness to perform Parton's work underscored a broader respect among rock musicians for the country artist's songwriting craft and emotional depth. White's career has been marked by his distinctive musical techniques and eccentricity, qualities that perhaps gave him the artistic credibility to interpret one of country music's most delicate compositions.

Parton's death represents the loss of a major cultural figure whose influence extended across generational and genre lines. Artists like White, who might seem removed from country music's mainstream, felt compelled to honor her publicly, suggesting the depth of her legacy in popular music. The London concert tribute became a moment of collective recognition of Parton's immeasurable contributions to music and culture.
