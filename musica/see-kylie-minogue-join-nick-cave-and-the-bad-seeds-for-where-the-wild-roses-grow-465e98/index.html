See Kylie Minogue Join Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds for ‘Where the Wild Roses Grow’ at Brighton Show

What's happening

Kylie Minogue joined Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds onstage in Brighton to perform their 1997 duet 'Where the Wild Roses Grow.' The performance marks the first time the Australian music icons have reunited for the song since 2019.

Who's involved

Kylie Minogue is an Australian pop icon frequently called the 'Princess of Pop,' with over 80 million records sold worldwide and accolades including two Grammy Awards and four Brit Awards. Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds is an Australian rock band formed in Melbourne in 1983, led by vocalist Nick Cave alongside multi-instrumentalist Warren Ellis and other longtime band members.

Why it matters

The reunion represents a rare onstage collaboration between two major Australian music legends and their most significant joint performance in five years. For fans of both artists, the moment celebrates the enduring legacy of 'Murder Ballads,' the 1996 album that introduced their haunting duet to audiences worldwide.

The story

Kylie Minogue and Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds reunited on stage at a Brighton show, bringing together two of Australia's most celebrated musical exports for a performance of 'Where the Wild Roses Grow.' The collaboration marks their first time performing the song together since 2019, according to Rolling Stone, reuniting the artists who created one of alternative rock's most haunting duets.

'Where the Wild Roses Grow' appears on Cave's 1996 album 'Murder Ballads,' a project that paired the Bad Seeds' dark, gothic sensibility with Minogue's contrasting pop sensibility to create a distinctly theatrical narrative song. The track became one of the most recognizable collaborations between an alternative rock act and a mainstream pop artist, blending Cave's gravelly baritone with Minogue's refined vocal delivery across lyrics that tell an ominous, cinematic story.

Kylie Minogue has established herself as the highest-selling Australian-born female artist of all time, achieving recognition across both music and fashion as a major style icon. Her career has spanned decades and multiple genres, earning her two Grammy Awards, four Brit Awards, and eighteen ARIA Music Awards. Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds, formed in Melbourne in 1983, have maintained their status as influential figures in rock music, with Cave's songwriting and the band's instrumental arrangements continuing to shape alternative rock aesthetics.

The Brighton performance underscores the artistic partnership that has defined both artists' willingness to explore unexpected collaborations. While Minogue's career has predominantly focused on pop and dance music, and Cave's work remains rooted in rock and experimental songwriting, their shared Australian heritage and mutual respect have allowed them to create moments that transcend genre boundaries. Previous reunions for the song have been infrequent, making each performance a noteworthy event for audiences invested in both artists' legacies.

The timing of this reunion comes as both artists continue active careers—Minogue remains a fixture in pop music and touring, while Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds continue to perform and record new material. For fans present at the Brighton show, the performance offered a rare glimpse of these two icons performing one of their most significant collaborative works, a song that has endured as a standout moment in both of their artistic catalogs.
