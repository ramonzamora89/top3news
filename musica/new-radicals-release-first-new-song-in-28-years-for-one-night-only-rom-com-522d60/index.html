New Radicals Release First New Song in 28 Years for ‘One Night Only’ Rom-Com

What's happening

New Radicals, the alternative rock band best known for their 1998 hit "You Get What You Give," have released their first new song in 28 years. The track, titled "One Night Only (Break Loose Break Free!)," appears on the soundtrack for an upcoming romantic comedy film.

Who's involved

New Radicals is an American alternative rock band formed in 1997 in Los Angeles, centered on the duo of Gregg Alexander and Danielle Brisebois. The band achieved major success with their 1998 album "You Get What You Give" before going on hiatus.

Why it matters

The release marks a significant moment for the band's fanbase, who have waited nearly three decades for new material. The placement on a major film soundtrack demonstrates continued commercial viability for the group and suggests potential for further activity after an extended absence from recording.

The story

New Radicals have returned to the studio after nearly three decades away, delivering their first new song since 1998. The track, "One Night Only (Break Loose Break Free!)," is featured on the soundtrack for an upcoming romantic comedy film, according to Rolling Stone.

New Radicals, formed in 1997 in Los Angeles, became a defining voice of 1990s alternative rock through the duo of Gregg Alexander and Danielle Brisebois, supplemented by session and touring musicians. Their breakthrough came with the 1998 album "You Get What You Give," which featured their signature hit single of the same name. The song became a cultural touchstone and remains one of the most recognizable tracks from that era.

The band's activity in the intervening decades has been limited, making the appearance of fresh material a notable event. The decision to introduce new music through a film soundtrack is a strategic choice that places the song in front of a wide audience while maintaining the band's selective approach to releases.

The rom-com placement suggests the new track may follow a more accessible, pop-oriented direction consistent with the band's breakthrough sound. Details about the film itself and any potential plans for additional New Radicals material remain unclear. However, the release indicates that the core members have reunited creatively, opening questions about whether this marks the beginning of a broader return or remains a one-off project.

For longtime fans of New Radicals, the new song represents a concrete connection to a band that defined much of their 1990s soundtrack. The 28-year gap between releases underscores how significant the moment is for a group that essentially disappeared from public consciousness after their initial run.
