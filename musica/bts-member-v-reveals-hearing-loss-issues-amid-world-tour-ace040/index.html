BTS Member V Reveals Hearing Loss Issues Amid World Tour

What's happening

BTS member V has disclosed significant hearing loss in his right ear during a livestream with fans, revealing that his right ear functions at only 30 percent compared to his left ear's full capacity. The revelation comes as the seven-member group continues their world tour activities.

Who's involved

V (Kim Tae-hyung) is a South Korean singer who debuted with BTS in 2013 under Big Hit Entertainment. BTS, also known as the Bangtan Boys, is a globally prominent South Korean boy band formed in 2010 that has expanded from hip-hop roots to incorporate multiple genres while building a massive international fanbase.

Why it matters

Hearing loss raises concerns about V's long-term health and ability to perform at the level expected during an active world tour, potentially affecting both his career trajectory and the band's schedule. The disclosure also highlights occupational health risks for professional musicians and performers who face prolonged exposure to loud environments during rehearsals, recording, and live performances.

The story

BTS member V revealed Wednesday during a livestream conversation with fans that he is experiencing significant hearing loss, disclosing that his right ear functions at only 30 percent capacity compared to his left ear. The candid revelation marks a rare public acknowledgment of a health challenge faced by one of the world's most in-demand performers, according to Rolling Stone.

V, whose real name is Kim Tae-hyung, made his debut as a member of BTS in 2013 and has since become one of the most recognizable figures in the band. Since their formation in 2010, BTS has grown into a global phenomenon, with the seven-member group co-writing and co-producing much of their material while maintaining an intensive schedule of performances, recordings, and promotional activities across multiple continents.

The timing of V's disclosure is particularly significant given that BTS is actively engaged in world tour commitments. Professional musicians, especially those performing in large venues with amplified sound systems, face considerable occupational exposure to noise levels that can damage hearing over time. The cumulative effect of years spent in rehearsal studios, recording sessions, and live performance environments can contribute to progressive hearing degradation.

V is not the first musician to publicly address hearing issues related to his profession. The revelation underscores the physical toll that sustained performance careers can exact, even on artists in their prime years. For a member of one of the world's most successful acts, managing such a condition while maintaining performance standards presents both personal and professional challenges.

The livestream disclosure suggests V felt comfortable sharing this health information directly with the BTS fanbase, known for their devoted support. How the hearing loss will affect his upcoming performances and whether any adjustments to tour schedules or in-ear monitoring systems may be necessary remains unclear at this time.
