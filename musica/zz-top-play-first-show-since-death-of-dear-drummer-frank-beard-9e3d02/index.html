ZZ Top Play First Show Since Death of ‘Dear Drummer’ Frank Beard

What's happening

ZZ Top returned to the stage in Austin for their first performance since the death of drummer Frank Beard earlier this week. Billy Gibbons told the crowd they were gathered for a celebration, signaling the band's determination to honor their late bandmate by continuing to perform.

Who's involved

ZZ Top is an iconic American rock band formed in Houston in 1969, consisting of vocalist-guitarist Billy Gibbons, drummer Frank Beard, and bassist-vocalist Dusty Hill until Hill's death in 2021. Beard, who passed away in 2026 after five decades with the band, was a cornerstone of their signature blues-based sound.

Why it matters

Beard's death marks a watershed moment for one of rock's most enduring acts, bringing an end to an era of nearly uninterrupted lineup stability spanning five decades. The band's immediate return to performing signals their commitment to their legacy and fanbase, even as they face the challenge of continuing without a founding member.

The story

ZZ Top played their first show since the death of drummer Frank Beard, taking the stage in Austin as Billy Gibbons addressed the crowd with a message of unity and remembrance. "We're all here for a celebration, you know what's going on. So we're gonna have a good time," Gibbons told the audience, according to Rolling Stone, setting the tone for a performance dedicated to honoring their late bandmate.

Beard's passing this week marked the end of an extraordinary chapter in rock history. The drummer had been with ZZ Top since the band's formation in Houston, Texas, in 1969, making him a foundational element of their sound for nearly five decades. Along with bassist-vocalist Dusty Hill, Beard formed the rhythm section that anchored Billy Gibbons' distinctive blues-influenced guitar work, helping ZZ Top develop the signature sound that made them one of rock's most recognizable acts.

The band's lineup had remained remarkably stable throughout their career until recent years. Dusty Hill's death in 2021 was the first significant change to the trio's composition, and Beard's death in 2026 represents the loss of the final surviving founding member alongside Gibbons. The drummer was known not only for his technical contributions but also for being an integral part of the band's visual identity and stage presence.

ZZ Top built their reputation over decades on a foundation of live performances, sly and humorous lyrics, and a carefully cultivated image that became iconic in rock culture. Beard's role as the "dear drummer," as he was affectionately referred to, was essential to the chemistry that made their concerts legendary. His passing leaves Gibbons navigating the future of the band at a pivotal moment.

By returning to Austin so quickly after Beard's death, ZZ Top demonstrated their commitment to their fans and their legacy. The choice to frame the performance as a celebration rather than a somber farewell reflects the band's approach to honoring their late drummer while maintaining the spirit of live rock and roll that has defined their career. For longtime fans and the Austin community where the band originated, this performance represented both a time of grief and a reaffirmation of the enduring impact of ZZ Top's music.
