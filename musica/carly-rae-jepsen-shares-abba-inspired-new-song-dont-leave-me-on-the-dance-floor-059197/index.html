Carly Rae Jepsen Shares ABBA-Inspired New Song ‘Don’t Leave Me on the Dance Floor’

What's happening

Carly Rae Jepsen has released 'Don't Leave Me on the Dance Floor,' an ABBA-inspired single from the "Night" portion of her upcoming double album. The track arrives with a music video directed by Jeremy O. Harris and starring actor Alden Ehrenreich.

Who's involved

Carly Rae Jepsen is a Canadian singer and songwriter who rose to mainstream prominence after placing third on Canadian Idol in 2007. The song draws inspiration from ABBA, the Swedish pop group formed in 1972 that became one of history's most commercially successful musical acts.

Why it matters

The release marks a significant moment in Jepsen's continued artistic evolution and her engagement with disco and pop influences. The double-album format and high-profile creative collaborators signal ambition in her current project, while the ABBA homage reflects the enduring cultural relevance of the Swedish group's sound.

The story

Carly Rae Jepsen is sharing new music from her forthcoming double album, unveiling the disco-tinged single 'Don't Leave Me on the Dance Floor' as the first track from the "Night" portion of the project. According to Rolling Stone, the song arrives alongside a music video helmed by director Jeremy O. Harris, which features actor Alden Ehrenreich in a leading role.

The track wears its influences plainly in its title and sonic approach, drawing direct inspiration from ABBA, the legendary Swedish pop group that dominated the charts throughout the 1970s and 1980s. ABBA, formed in Stockholm in 1972 by Agnetha Fältskog, Björn Ulvaeus, Benny Andersson, and Anni-Frid Lyngstad, remains among the most renowned and commercially successful musical groups in history. By invoking their sound and sensibility, Jepsen positions herself within a lineage of pop craftsmanship that prioritizes infectious melodies and polished production.

Jepsen, who is a Canadian singer and songwriter, first gained widespread attention after placing third on the fifth season of Canadian Idol in 2007. Following that breakthrough, she released her debut studio album Tug of War the following year, establishing herself as an artist willing to explore different sonic territories. Over the course of her career, she has continued to refine her approach to pop songwriting and production.

The double-album structure represents an ambitious undertaking, with the "Night" installment housing 'Don't Leave Me on the Dance Floor' alongside other yet-to-be-revealed material. This framework allows Jepsen to explore contrasting moods and themes across the two halves of the project, a format that reflects a deliberate artistic vision.

The involvement of Jeremy O. Harris as the director for the accompanying visual content underscores the caliber of collaborators surrounding the project. Harris, known for his creative direction work across music and entertainment, brings a distinctive visual sensibility to the track's presentation.
