Watch Van Halen’s Michael Anthony Play ‘Runnin’ With The Devil’ With Jason Bonham

What's happening

Michael Anthony, bassist for Van Halen, performed 'Runnin' With The Devil'—a Van Halen classic—alongside Jason Bonham at one of Bonham's Led Zeppelin tribute shows. It marked a historic first: the 16-year-old Led Zeppelin Evening had never featured a song outside the Zeppelin catalog until this performance.

Who's involved

Michael Anthony is the bassist for Van Halen, the legendary American rock band formed in Pasadena in 1972 and credited with restoring hard rock to mainstream prominence. Jason Bonham is the son of John Bonham, Led Zeppelin's original drummer, and has spent 16 years headlining the Led Zeppelin Evening, a tribute show celebrating his father's legendary band.

Why it matters

The performance represents a rare departure from the strict setlist boundaries that have defined Jason Bonham's Led Zeppelin Evening for nearly two decades, signaling a willingness to expand the show's scope. It also underscores the shared musical DNA between Van Halen and Led Zeppelin, two foundational acts in rock history whose combined influence shaped generations of musicians.

The story

Michael Anthony brought a piece of Van Halen's catalog to Jason Bonham's Led Zeppelin Evening when he joined the tribute show to perform 'Runnin' With The Devil,' marking a watershed moment in the 16-year history of Bonham's traveling exhibition of his father's legendary material. According to Rolling Stone, the performance was the first time a song outside the Led Zeppelin catalog had been featured at one of these events.

Jason Bonham has spent the better part of two decades curating these shows as a celebration of his father, John Bonham, who was the drummer for Led Zeppelin from the band's 1968 formation in London through their 1980 dissolution. Led Zeppelin, comprising vocalist Robert Plant, guitarist Jimmy Page, bassist and keyboardist John Paul Jones, and John Bonham on drums, pioneered the synthesis of heavy electric rock with blues and folk influences that became the template for hard rock and heavy metal itself. Their cultural footprint remains immense—they are among the best-selling music artists of all time.

Van Halen, formed in Pasadena, California in 1972, carved out their own towering legacy by restoring hard rock to the forefront of the music scene during an era when the genre needed reinvention. The band became known for electrifying live performances and the virtuosic guitar work of Eddie Van Halen, whose technical innovations influenced countless players. 'Runnin' With The Devil,' one of Van Halen's signature tracks, represents the hard-hitting energy and musicianship that defined their career.

Michael Anthony's appearance at the Led Zeppelin Evening underscores an implicit kinship between the two bands. Both Van Halen and Led Zeppelin occupied the upper echelon of rock's power hierarchy, commanding massive audiences and shaping the sound of their respective eras. By stepping onto the stage to perform Van Halen material within the framework of Bonham's tribute, Anthony created a symbolic convergence of two lineages that, while distinct in style, share common roots in blues-inflected hard rock and virtuoso musicianship.

The decision to break the Led Zeppelin-only tradition suggests that Jason Bonham may be open to occasional guest appearances and musical detours that honor the broader rock pantheon while maintaining the show's primary focus. Such moments are rare enough in a long-running tribute series that they become memorable for fans and musicians alike, creating unexpected connections between artists and eras that shaped rock history.
