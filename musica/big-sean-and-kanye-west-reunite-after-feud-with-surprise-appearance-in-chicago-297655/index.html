Big Sean and Kanye West Reunite After Feud With Surprise Appearance in Chicago

What's happening

Big Sean made a surprise appearance at Kanye West's homecoming concert at Soldier Field in Chicago, reuniting the two collaborators after their well-documented feud. The pair performed several of their collaborative hits together, marking a significant reconciliation in hip-hop.

Who's involved

Big Sean is a rapper who signed to Kanye West's GOOD Music label in 2007 and rose to prominence through the 2010s with chart-topping albums. Kanye West is a rapper, songwriter, and producer widely regarded as one of hip-hop's greatest figures, known for his stylistic innovation and polarizing public persona.

Why it matters

The reunion signals a potential end to a high-profile feud between two major figures in hip-hop and suggests the possibility of future collaborations between artists with significant shared history. For Kanye's Chicago homecoming event, having Big Sean perform carries symbolic weight—a validation of the local artist's legacy and a gesture toward healing creative relationships within the industry.

The story

Big Sean joined Kanye West on stage at Soldier Field in Chicago on Tuesday night for a surprise reunion that thrilled fans and marked a turning point in the two rappers' fractured relationship. The duo performed several of their most celebrated collaborations, including "Clique," "Mercy," and "I Don't Fuck With You," according to Rolling Stone. The appearance came as part of Kanye's homecoming concert, a significant cultural moment for the Chicago-born producer returning to perform at one of the city's most iconic venues.

Big Sean and Kanye West have a history that spans nearly two decades. The two first connected when Big Sean was a teenager, and in 2007, Sean Michael Leonard Anderson—known professionally as Big Sean—signed with West's GOOD Music label, an imprint of Def Jam Recordings. Big Sean's career trajectory accelerated following the release of his third mixtape, Finally Famous Vol. 3: Big, in 2010, followed by his debut studio album Finally Famous in 2011. Both of his first two studio albums, Finally Famous and Hall of Fame, peaked at number three on the Billboard 200 and spawned multiple chart-topping singles, establishing him as one of rap's most commercially viable artists.

Their collaborations became some of hip-hop's most memorable tracks during the early 2010s, with "Clique" and "Mercy" standing out as particularly influential collaborations that showcased the chemistry between the two artists. However, like many creative relationships in the industry, the partnership had cooled in recent years, with public tensions surfacing that left fans wondering whether the two would ever work together again.

Kanye West, who has been listed among the greatest rappers of all time and frequently credited as one of hip-hop's most prominent figures, has built a career on stylistic reinvention and cultural commentary—though his public persona has also generated significant controversy. His music has been credited with facilitating the emergence of rappers who rejected the gangsta rap conventions that dominated much of the genre's commercial landscape.

The Soldier Field appearance represents more than just a nostalgic performance. For Kanye, bringing Big Sean on stage at his Chicago homecoming concert signals an acknowledgment of their shared legacy and suggests openness to healing past tensions. For Big Sean, the reunion provides visibility and validation of his contributions to some of the decade's most important hip-hop records. Whether this moment leads to future collaborations or simply serves as a one-time reconciliation remains to be seen, but it demonstrates that even fractured relationships between major artists can be mended.
