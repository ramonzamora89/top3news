Radiohead Reveal 2027 Japan and Australia Tour

What's happening

Radiohead has announced a 21-date tour across Japan and Australia scheduled for 2027, continuing the English rock band's live comeback that began with European reunion dates in fall 2025.

Who's involved

Radiohead, the Oxford-formed rock band featuring Thom Yorke, brothers Jonny and Colin Greenwood, Ed O'Brien, and Philip Selway, are mounting their most expansive tour itinerary in years. The band has been known for their experimental approach to alternative rock since their 1985 formation.

Why it matters

The tour signals Radiohead's sustained commitment to live performance after years of inactivity, potentially regenerating interest in the band across major markets and offering fans their first opportunity to see the group in Asia-Pacific in over a decade. The scale of the announcement—spanning two continents with 21 dates—suggests confidence in demand and marks a significant moment for a band that has largely focused on studio work and solo projects in recent years.

The story

Radiohead has revealed plans for an extensive 2027 tour of Japan and Australia, bringing the acclaimed English rock band to two key international markets. According to Rolling Stone, the 21-date trek will follow the band's previously announced fall 2025 reunion dates in Europe, marking the next phase of what appears to be a substantial live touring cycle.

The band, which formed in Abingdon, Oxfordshire, in 1985, has undergone numerous transformations since their breakthrough in the 1990s. The five-member lineup—Thom Yorke on vocals, brothers Jonny Greenwood and Colin Greenwood on guitar and bass respectively, Ed O'Brien on guitar, and Philip Selway on drums—has built a reputation for experimental approaches to alternative rock that pushed the genre forward across multiple decades. Their consistent working relationship with producer Nigel Godrich and cover artist Stanley Donwood since 1994 has been central to their distinctive visual and sonic identity.

The announcement of dates across Japan and Australia suggests strong international demand for Radiohead's live performances. These regions represent major markets for the band, and the extended tour structure indicates the group is preparing for a sustained return to the road rather than a one-off reunion. The timing, coming after European reunion shows in 2025, allows the band to build momentum across multiple continents while giving fans in Asia-Pacific access to performances they have been denied during the group's long hiatus from touring.

For Radiohead fans, the tour represents a rare opportunity to experience the band live in an era when major reunions remain noteworthy events in rock music. The 21-date span across two countries underscores the scale of interest in the group's return, positioning 2027 as a significant year for alternative rock tourism and live music events in the region.
