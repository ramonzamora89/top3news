See King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard Bring Out Max Weinberg at Queens Show

What's happening

Max Weinberg, the legendary E Street Band drummer, took the stage at King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard's Forest Hills Stadium concert in Queens, stepping in as a special guest. The iconic drummer filled in for Jay Weinberg, his son and a friend of the Australian psych-rock band.

Who's involved

Max Weinberg is an American drummer best known as Bruce Springsteen's longtime E Street Band drummer and as bandleader for Conan O'Brien's late-night television shows. King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard is an Australian rock band formed in Melbourne in 2010, known for their energetic live performances and prolific output across multiple genres.

Why it matters

The appearance represents a rare cross-generational and cross-genre collaboration, bringing together a classic rock legend with a contemporary psych-rock act. It highlights the continuing reach and influence of established musicians while underscoring the collaborative spirit within modern rock communities.

The story

Max Weinberg, the drummer synonymous with Bruce Springsteen's E Street Band for decades, made a surprise appearance at King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard's Queens show, bringing his formidable presence to the Australian psych-rockers' Forest Hills Stadium concert. According to Rolling Stone, Weinberg filled in for Jay Weinberg, his son and a friend of the band, in a moment that bridged classic and contemporary rock worlds.

King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard, formed in Melbourne in 2010, has built a reputation as one of rock's most dynamic live acts, known for staging energetic performances and exploring a wide range of genres across their prolific catalog. The band's current lineup includes Stu Mackenzie, Ambrose Kenny-Smith, Cook Craig, Joey Walker, Lucas Harwood, and Michael Cavanagh. Their willingness to invite special guests to their performances reflects the collaborative ethos that has defined their approach to live music.

Max Weinberg's appearance carried particular significance given his decades-long tenure as the heartbeat of the E Street Band. Beyond his work with Springsteen, Weinberg has maintained a high profile as a television personality, serving as bandleader for Conan O'Brien on both Late Night and The Tonight Show. His participation in the Forest Hills show demonstrated the kind of spontaneous collaboration that energizes live rock performances and creates memorable moments for audiences.

Jay Weinberg, Max's son, has established his own drumming credentials, having worked with both Slipknot and Suicidal Tendencies. His friendship with King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard, strong enough to make him a regular part of their live performances, created the circumstances for Max's guest appearance. The intergenerational dynamic—with the elder Weinberg stepping in for his son—added an extra layer of meaning to an already notable moment.

The Queens concert showcased the kind of cross-genre pollination that continues to define rock music in the contemporary landscape, where established legends and emerging acts frequently find common ground on stage. For audiences at Forest Hills Stadium, the evening provided an unexpected encounter with one of rock's most accomplished and enduring drummers.
