Google Earth’s AI deepfake tool only lasted one day

What's happening

Google has shut down an AI image-editing feature for Google Earth that it launched just one day prior. The tool allowed users to generate deepfakes of real-world satellite imagery by typing text prompts, but the company pulled it almost immediately.

Who's involved

Google, the tech giant behind Google Earth—a 3D mapping platform that overlays satellite imagery, aerial photography, and GIS data onto a virtual globe to let users explore Earth from various angles. The company has been experimenting with AI-powered creative tools across its product line.

Why it matters

The rapid shutdown signals potential concerns about AI deepfakes of real-world locations and the risks of synthetic media being generated at scale. It highlights the tension between innovation and safety as companies test new AI capabilities without fully anticipating misuse or public backlash. The incident may foreshadow how quickly experimental AI features could face regulatory or reputational pressure.

The story

Google launched and then rapidly withdrew an artificial intelligence feature for Google Earth on Thursday that enabled users to edit satellite imagery through simple text prompts. The tool, which essentially allowed the creation of AI-generated deepfakes of real-world locations, was removed after just one day—a stark indicator of the company's caution around the technology's potential risks.

Google Earth, a web and computer program that renders a 3D representation of Earth by superimposing satellite images, aerial photography, and GIS data onto a 3D globe, had been positioned as a platform for exploration and discovery. The new AI editing capability appeared to expand that mission by letting users reimagine real locations. One example demonstrated by the company showed Pompeii reimagined using the tool, showcasing how dramatically the feature could alter recognizable landmarks.

Deepfakes—images, videos, or audio edited or generated using artificial intelligence tools—have become an increasingly contentious issue across the tech industry. While they can serve creative purposes, deepfakes also carry significant risks for misinformation, fraud, and manipulation. By integrating deepfake creation directly into one of its most widely used mapping products, Google appeared to be crossing into territory it ultimately deemed too sensitive to maintain.

According to The Verge, the company's decision to pull the feature after a single day of availability suggests either unexpected user backlash, internal concerns about misuse, or both. The speed of the shutdown indicates that Google may not have fully anticipated how the tool would be received or the ways it could be weaponized to generate false or misleading imagery of real-world locations.

The incident underscores the challenges facing companies developing generative AI tools. While innovation in AI capabilities continues to accelerate, the pathway from launch to public deployment remains fraught with potential pitfalls. A feature that seemed promising in development can quickly become a liability once users gain access to it and creative—or malicious—applications emerge.
