Chess.com launched a poker site and is planning even more classic games

What's happening

Chess.com, the world's dominant online chess platform, has quietly launched Gambit, a free poker website, signaling its expansion beyond chess into other classic games. The company is planning to add even more games to its portfolio in the coming months.

Who's involved

Chess.com is the largest chess platform in the world, operating on a freemium model with millions of users playing live chess in various time controls, analyzing games, and learning from educational resources. The Verge reports on this strategic pivot.

Why it matters

Chess.com's diversification could reshape how players view the company—transforming it from a single-game specialist into a broader gaming platform. Success with poker and additional games could unlock new revenue streams and user bases, while risking dilution of the brand identity that made Chess.com the market leader in online chess.

The story

Chess.com has spent nearly two decades establishing itself as the global standard for online chess, but the company is now venturing into unfamiliar territory. The platform quietly launched Gambit, its first companion site, in beta back in May. Gambit is a free online poker website designed to help players learn the game, marking Chess.com's first major expansion beyond the 64 squares that made it famous.

Chess.com's core offering has remained tightly focused since its inception. The site operates on a freemium model where basic features—including live online chess games against other users in daily, rapid, blitz, and bullet time controls—are available for free, while advanced features and premium analysis tools require a subscription. The platform also offers play against chess engines, computer analysis, chess puzzles, and teaching resources. This singular focus has helped Chess.com become the largest chess platform in the world, attracting millions of players worldwide.

The launch of Gambit suggests Chess.com's leadership believes there is untapped potential in parlaying its reputation and user base into adjacent gaming categories. According to The Verge, the company is planning even more classic games beyond poker, though specific titles and timelines have not been announced. The approach mirrors successful gaming platforms that have expanded from strength in one category into others.

Poker presents a natural complement to chess in some ways—both involve strategic decision-making, reading opponents, and managing probability. However, the games appeal to different skill sets and player psychologies. Poker is a family of comparing card games in which players wager over which hand is best, played worldwide with varying rules and deck configurations. The learning curve and community dynamics differ significantly from chess, meaning Chess.com will need to build distinct expertise and culture for Gambit if it hopes to compete in poker's crowded online marketplace.

The beta launch in May, if indeed quiet, suggests Chess.com is testing the waters carefully before making any major commitment. Whether Gambit and its planned successors will attract the devoted, competitive communities that Chess.com cultivated in chess remains an open question. The company's decision to remain freemium across platforms suggests a bet that user acquisition and engagement matter more than immediate monetization in emerging categories.
