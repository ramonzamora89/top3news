Uber beats Waymo as first to launch robotaxis in London

What's happening

Uber has launched the first commercial robotaxi service in London, beating Waymo to market. The vehicles are powered by autonomous driving technology developed by Wayve, a UK-based startup, and will initially operate with safety drivers on board.

Who's involved

Uber, the ride-hailing giant, partnered with Wayve Technologies Ltd, a London-headquartered autonomous driving startup founded in 2017 by University of Cambridge researchers. Waymo, Alphabet's autonomous vehicle subsidiary, had been expected to launch in London first but was beaten to the milestone by Uber.

Why it matters

This marks a significant milestone for Uber's autonomous vehicle ambitions and validates Wayve's alternative approach to self-driving technology. The London launch represents a major step toward mainstream robotaxi adoption in the UK and demonstrates that non-US companies and approaches can compete with established players like Waymo, which already operates services across 10 US metropolitan areas.

The story

Uber has achieved a major milestone by launching London's first commercial robotaxi service, outpacing Waymo in the race to bring autonomous vehicles to the British capital. The vehicles rely on autonomous driving technology developed by Wayve, a Cambridge-based startup that has taken a distinctive approach to self-driving systems using end-to-end deep learning rather than detailed 3D maps and hand-coded rules.

Wayve, founded in 2017 by researchers from the University of Cambridge, has garnered significant attention and funding for its visually-based method of teaching vehicles to drive themselves. The company's "AI driver" learns from camera data and real-world driving experience, allowing it to adapt to new environments without requiring the extensive pre-mapped infrastructure that traditional autonomous systems demand. This approach has set Wayve apart in a competitive field dominated by established players with vastly larger resources.

According to The Verge, the initial London rollout will feature safety drivers behind the wheel, a common practice during the early deployment phases of robotaxi services. This staged approach allows Uber and Wayve to gather operational data and build public confidence in the technology before moving toward fully driverless operations. The launch represents validation of Wayve's technology on a commercial scale and signals growing competition in the autonomous vehicle market beyond the United States.

Waymo, Alphabet's autonomous vehicle subsidiary, had been widely expected to lead in launching a London robotaxi service. Waymo operates public commercial robotaxi services in 10 US metropolitan areas and has demonstrated substantial scale, with 3,871 robotaxis in service and 500,000 paid rides per week as of mid-2026. However, Uber's partnership with Wayve has allowed the ride-hailing company to move quickly and leverage localized expertise.

The London launch underscores the growing maturity of autonomous vehicle technology and the emergence of alternative technical approaches beyond the dominant paradigm. Rather than competing solely on computing power and mapping resources, Wayve's learning-based model offers a pathway for faster adaptation to new cities and regulatory environments. For Uber, the service provides an opportunity to integrate autonomous vehicles into its existing ride-hailing operations and gather evidence of profitability and customer acceptance in a major global market.
