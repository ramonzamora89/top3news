Raspberry Pi shares its official tutorial for making a cyberdeck

What's happening

Raspberry Pi has officially embraced the cyberdeck trend by releasing a tutorial for building one, acknowledging that these custom portable computers have become impossible to ignore in the maker community. The company's head of social media noted that cyberdecks have dominated conversations around the platform throughout the year.

Who's involved

Raspberry Pi, a UK-based manufacturer of small single-board computers (SBCs) originally developed by the Raspberry Pi Foundation in collaboration with Broadcom, is taking an official stance on the cyberdeck movement. Ashley Whittaker, Raspberry Pi's head of social, is the company representative quoted on the trend.

Why it matters

By releasing an official cyberdeck tutorial, Raspberry Pi is legitimizing a grassroots maker movement while capitalizing on growing demand for customizable portable computing devices. This move signals that niche hardware trends can transition into mainstream recognition when they reach critical mass in the enthusiast community.

The story

Raspberry Pi, the UK-based manufacturer of single-board computers, has officially weighed in on one of this year's biggest maker trends: cyberdecks. According to The Verge, Ashley Whittaker, Raspberry Pi's head of social, acknowledged the cyberdeck phenomenon by saying the company "hasn't been able to get away from cyberdecks this year."

A cyberdeck is a custom-built, portable personal computer typically designed for individualized purposes and featuring a cyberpunk aesthetic. These devices are individually crafted by makers using laptop motherboards or single-board computers like Raspberry Pis, and they incorporate displays, keyboards, and housings made from thrifted or recycled materials such as purses, jewelry boxes, and other repurposed objects. The aesthetic appeals to a growing community of DIY computer enthusiasts who view computing hardware as a canvas for creative expression.

Raspberry Pi's decision to publish an official tutorial represents a significant shift in how the company engages with grassroots maker culture. By providing guidance on cyberdeck construction, the company is not simply acknowledging the trend—it is actively participating in and legitimizing it. This move transforms cyberdecks from an underground niche hobby into something with corporate backing and institutional support.

The timing reflects the rapid acceleration of the cyberdeck movement within the maker community. What began as scattered individual projects by hobbyists has evolved into a recognizable subculture with its own aesthetics, communities, and shared design principles. Raspberry Pi's involvement suggests the trend has reached a tipping point where ignoring it was no longer feasible for a company whose products are at the core of many of these builds.

For Raspberry Pi Holdings, the company's commercial entity, this tutorial represents both a marketing opportunity and a genuine engagement with its user base. Single-board computers have always been positioned as educational and hackable devices, and cyberdecks embody exactly that spirit—transforming commodity hardware into something personalized and unique. By providing official guidance, Raspberry Pi strengthens its brand identity as a platform for creative makers rather than just a component supplier.
