Two great new repairable gadgets

What's happening

The Verge's Installer newsletter has highlighted two new gadgets that prioritize repairability, a growing consumer concern. The featured products represent a shift toward devices designed to be fixed rather than replaced.

Who's involved

The Verge, a technology and culture publication, curates consumer gadget recommendations through its Installer newsletter series. The newsletter, now in its 141st edition, serves as a guide to notable technology products and consumer goods.

Why it matters

The spotlight on repairable gadgets reflects increasing consumer demand for sustainable technology and reduced electronic waste. As manufacturers face pressure to extend product lifecycles, repairability has become a competitive differentiator in the consumer electronics market, affecting both environmental impact and long-term ownership costs.

The story

Consumer electronics have long followed a pattern of planned obsolescence, with manufacturers designing products meant to be discarded rather than repaired. But a new wave of gadgets is challenging that approach by prioritizing durability and user-friendly fixes. According to The Verge, this week's Installer newsletter features two standout products that exemplify this shift toward repairability.

The move toward repairable gadgets addresses a mounting problem: electronic waste. When devices cannot be easily serviced or upgraded, consumers face the choice of either living with degraded performance or purchasing replacements entirely. This cycle has contributed to millions of tons of e-waste annually, filled with valuable materials and toxic components. By designing products that users can repair themselves or service through accessible channels, manufacturers can significantly extend the useful life of their goods.

The Installer series has built its reputation on identifying products that matter to consumers seeking quality and thoughtful design. The newsletter's 141st edition continues this tradition while reflecting broader market changes. Tech companies increasingly recognize that repairability appeals to environmentally conscious consumers and can reduce support costs through customer self-service repairs. Right-to-repair movements have also pressured manufacturers to make spare parts and repair documentation more accessible.

These two featured gadgets represent different product categories, yet share a common philosophy: devices should be serviceable throughout their lifespan. Whether through modular designs that allow component swaps, easily replaceable batteries, or straightforward access to internal parts, repairable products reduce the barrier between a malfunctioning device and a working one. This approach benefits consumers economically while supporting sustainability goals.

As consumer awareness of e-waste and product lifecycles grows, repairability is becoming a key purchasing criterion alongside traditional factors like performance and price. The gadgets highlighted in this week's Installer stand as evidence that the market is responding to these shifting priorities, with manufacturers investing in designs that prove devices can be both innovative and lasting.
