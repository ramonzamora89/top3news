Google further buries search results under AI mode

What's happening

Google is automatically expanding its AI-generated search summaries at the top of search results pages for some queries, pushing traditional link-based results significantly lower. This change represents a shift in how Google displays information, prioritizing AI-generated answers over the classic blue-link results that have defined the search experience for decades.

Who's involved

Google operates the world's most-visited search engine, which commands approximately 90% of the global search engine market as of 2025. The company has integrated generative AI into its search product to provide non-search responses alongside traditional results.

Why it matters

This change could fundamentally alter how users interact with search results and where web traffic flows, potentially disadvantaging websites that previously benefited from prominent link placement. For publishers and content creators, the shift raises concerns about visibility and traffic, while users may experience faster answers to certain queries but less exposure to diverse sources.

The story

Google is taking a more aggressive approach to its AI-powered search summaries, now automatically expanding them for certain queries in a way that significantly alters the layout of its search results page. According to Search Engine Roundtable, the expanded AI summaries now push the traditional list of links—the core element of Google Search for over two decades—much farther down the page. This represents an escalation from previous implementations where AI summaries appeared but remained more compact.

Google Search, which processes billions of queries daily and maintains a 90% share of the global search engine market, has increasingly incorporated generative AI capabilities to provide non-search responses alongside traditional web links. The integration of artificial intelligence—computational systems performing tasks like reasoning and problem-solving—allows Google to synthesize information and deliver direct answers rather than simply ranking and listing relevant web pages.

The practical impact of this change is immediate and visible. Users searching for information now encounter a more prominent block of AI-generated content before they see the hyperlinks to actual web pages. What was once a secondary element has become the primary focal point of many search results, fundamentally shifting the visual hierarchy of the search experience that users have grown accustomed to over the past 25 years.

For website publishers and content creators, this development poses a significant challenge. Traffic has historically flowed to sites that rank highly in Google's link-based results. With AI summaries now occupying prime real estate at the top of the page, the click-through traffic to individual websites may diminish, particularly for queries where Google's AI system provides comprehensive answers. Publishers may find their carefully optimized content buried beneath algorithmic summaries that synthesize information from multiple sources without driving users to visit those sources directly.

The expansion of AI summaries also reflects Google's broader business strategy during an era of increased competition from AI-powered search alternatives and chatbots. By making its AI capabilities more prominent and useful, Google aims to retain users within its ecosystem rather than risk them migrating to standalone AI tools. However, the trade-off involves reducing visibility for the web ecosystem that has historically benefited from Google Search traffic, creating tension between user experience improvements and the economic model that supports content creation across the internet.
