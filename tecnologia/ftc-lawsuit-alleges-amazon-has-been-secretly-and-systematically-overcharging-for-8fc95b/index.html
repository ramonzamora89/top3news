FTC lawsuit alleges Amazon has been ‘secretly and systematically’ overcharging for ads

What's happening

The FTC and 22 state attorneys general have filed a lawsuit against Amazon, alleging the company has been systematically overcharging advertisers through a hidden fee structure. The lawsuit claims Amazon used what amounts to a "secret ad surcharge" to artificially inflate advertising prices on its platform.

Who's involved

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC), an independent agency of the U.S. government whose principal mission is the enforcement of civil antitrust law and consumer protection, is leading the charge alongside 22 state attorneys general. Amazon, an American multinational technology and retail company, is the defendant being sued for its advertising practices.

Why it matters

If the allegations are substantiated, this case could reshape how major tech platforms price advertising services and set new precedents for FTC enforcement against dominant digital marketplaces. The lawsuit directly targets Amazon's business model in a high-growth segment and may signal more aggressive regulatory scrutiny of big tech's pricing practices going forward.

The story

Federal regulators are mounting a significant legal challenge to Amazon's advertising business, alleging that the company has engaged in systematic and undisclosed price manipulation. The FTC and 22 state attorneys general filed the lawsuit claiming Amazon imposed what amounted to a secret surcharge on advertisers using its platform, driving up the overall cost of digital advertising on Amazon's website and mobile app.

According to The Verge, FTC chairman Andrew Ferguson outlined the complaint in a blog post, stating that the artificially higher advertising prices "were largely passed on to" Amazon's retail customers. The allegation is that Amazon has operated this pricing scheme deliberately and without transparent disclosure to advertisers, effectively hiding the true cost structure behind what appeared to be standard ad rates.

The Federal Trade Commission, headquartered in the Federal Trade Commission Building in Washington, D.C., shares jurisdiction over federal civil antitrust law enforcement with the Antitrust Division of the U.S. Department of Justice. This lawsuit reflects the FTC's expanding focus on how dominant platforms leverage their market position to extract value from advertisers and, indirectly, from consumers who bear the cost through higher product prices.

Amazon's advertising business has become one of the company's most profitable segments in recent years, generating tens of billions of dollars annually. The alleged surcharge scheme would have allowed Amazon to increase margins on this business while maintaining the appearance of competitive pricing. The involvement of 22 state attorneys general alongside the federal regulator underscores the broad coalition of concern about Amazon's practices.

The lawsuit represents an escalation in regulatory pressure on Amazon, which has faced multiple antitrust investigations and complaints in recent years. If successful, the FTC's case could force Amazon to restructure how it charges for advertising services and potentially impose penalties for past conduct. The outcome may also influence how other major technology platforms structure their own advertising pricing and disclosure practices.
