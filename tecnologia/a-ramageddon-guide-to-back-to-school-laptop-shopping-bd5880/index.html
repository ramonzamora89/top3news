A RAMageddon guide to back-to-school laptop shopping

What's happening

Computer prices have become inflated due to ongoing RAM and storage shortages, making back-to-school laptop shopping challenging for budget-conscious students. The Verge is offering guidance on navigating the market, with the MacBook Neo highlighted as an accessible recommendation despite broader pricing pressures.

Who's involved

Apple, the computer manufacturer behind the MacBook line, is central to this story. The MacBook brand has been Apple's primary laptop offering since 2006, with the newer MacBook Neo entering the lineup in 2026 as part of the company's efforts to serve different market segments.

Why it matters

High laptop prices during back-to-school season directly affect students' ability to purchase necessary computing equipment for their studies. The RAM and storage crunch creating these inflated prices has broader implications for the entire consumer electronics market, potentially influencing purchasing decisions across educational institutions and households.

The story

Back-to-school season traditionally brings a surge in laptop purchases, but this year students and parents face an unusually difficult market. Computer prices have become significantly elevated due to ongoing shortages in RAM and storage components, creating what amounts to a supply-driven pricing crisis. The Verge reports that finding an affordable laptop requires careful shopping and realistic expectations about what features students can secure at reasonable price points.

Apple's laptop offerings have long dominated the education market. The MacBook brand, which replaced the PowerBook and iBook lines during Apple's transition to Intel processors in 2006, currently consists of the MacBook Pro, MacBook Air, and the newer MacBook Neo. These machines run macOS and serve different price tiers and user needs. According to The Verge, the MacBook Neo represents an easy recommendation for students seeking affordability, suggesting that Apple has positioned this newer entry-level model as a practical option even as component costs remain elevated.

The supply chain disruptions affecting RAM and storage components stem from broader semiconductor market dynamics. Random-access memory, which serves as the working storage for computer operations, has become particularly constrained. These components are fundamental to laptop functionality, storing both data and machine code that applications require during operation. When RAM and storage supplies tighten, manufacturers have limited ability to reduce prices, pushing costs to consumers.

For students shopping this season, the guidance appears to be twofold: accept that prices are higher than historical norms, but recognize that some options remain more reasonable than others. The MacBook Neo's emergence as a recommended choice suggests Apple has created a product specifically designed to address budget constraints during this period of elevated component costs. Students evaluating laptops should prioritize their actual needs rather than aspirational specifications, given that premium configurations will command premium prices in this constrained market.
