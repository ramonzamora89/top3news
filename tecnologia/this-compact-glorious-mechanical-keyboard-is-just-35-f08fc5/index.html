This compact Glorious mechanical keyboard is just $35

What's happening

Glorious is offering its GMMK 3 65-percent mechanical keyboard at a steep discount, bringing the price down to $35. The deal is aimed at consumers curious about custom mechanical keyboards who want to test the hobby without committing to a premium investment.

Who's involved

Glorious is a keyboard and gaming peripheral manufacturer known for producing customizable mechanical keyboards. The company has positioned itself as an accessible entry point for enthusiasts interested in the custom keyboard community.

Why it matters

This pricing removes a major barrier to entry for mechanical keyboard hobbyists, potentially expanding the customer base beyond those willing to spend hundreds of dollars upfront. For Glorious, the aggressive discount could drive volume sales and convert budget-conscious buyers into long-term customers who invest in premium switches, keycaps, and other accessories.

The story

Glorious is making custom mechanical keyboards significantly more accessible with a promotional price of just $35 for its GMMK 3 model. The 65-percent keyboard, according to The Verge, represents a rare opportunity for consumers hesitant about entering the custom keyboard market without first confirming their interest in the hobby.

Custom mechanical keyboards have grown into a niche but devoted community over the past decade, driven by enthusiasts seeking precision, durability, and personalization in their input devices. The design and manufacture of keyboards today involves multiple disciplines including engineering, ergonomics, materials science, and integrated circuit design, reflecting the complexity that has emerged in this seemingly simple product category. At full price, quality mechanical keyboards often cost $100 to $300 or more, creating a significant financial threshold for newcomers experimenting with different switch types, keycap profiles, and stabilizer configurations.

Glorious has built its reputation on offering customizable mechanical keyboards at various price points, though this $35 entry price is exceptionally low for the category. The GMMK 3 is positioned as a 65-percent keyboard, a size that sits between full-size and 60-percent models, offering a balance between functionality and desk real estate. The discounted version does not include the wrist rest shown in promotional imagery, a common trade-off in budget offerings.

For those evaluating whether to invest time and money into mechanical keyboard customization, the low barrier of entry makes experimentation more feasible. Buyers can purchase the base board and test it with their existing switches and keycaps, or gradually build out a collection of components without the sunk cost of a premium board from the outset. The strategy also allows Glorious to capture price-sensitive segments while introducing new users to its ecosystem.

The mechanical keyboard hobby has matured beyond simple functionality into a space where aesthetics, acoustics, and personalization drive purchasing decisions. This promotion demonstrates how manufacturers are adapting their pricing strategies to broaden market participation and convert casual interest into sustained engagement with premium product tiers.
