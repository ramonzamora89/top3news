No Dogs in Space is a music history podcast for true obsessives

What's happening

The music history podcast No Dogs in Space has gone silent, with no new episodes since July 2024. The show's third season, which focused on experimental rock and included a multi-part series on the German band Can, ended in an abbreviated form.

Who's involved

No Dogs in Space is a deep-dive music history podcast aimed at dedicated music obsessives and fans of experimental and alternative rock. The podcast recently covered Can, the pioneering German experimental rock band formed in Cologne in 1968 by Holger Czukay, Irmin Schmidt, Michael Karoli, and Jaki Liebezeit.

Why it matters

The hiatus raises questions about the sustainability of long-form music history podcasts and whether listener support can sustain ambitious, research-heavy shows. For fans of experimental music history and Can scholarship, the pause interrupts what appeared to be a comprehensive examination of one of krautrock's most influential acts.

The story

No Dogs in Space, a podcast dedicated to exploring music history with the kind of granular detail that appeals to true obsessives, has not released a new episode since July 2024. According to The Verge, the show's third season—which concentrated on experimental rock—concluded with part four of a series examining Can, the seminal German krautrock pioneers. The abbreviated season left fans wondering about the podcast's future and the factors behind the production pause.

Can, formed in Cologne in 1968, featured a rotating lineup of musicians anchored by keyboardist Irmin Schmidt, guitarist Michael Karoli, and drummer Jaki Liebezeit, with founding bassist Holger Czukay. The band became known for featuring multiple vocalists, including American Malcolm Mooney and Japanese Damo Suzuki across different eras, and helped establish the krautrock sound that influenced generations of experimental and rock musicians. A deep historical dive into the band's catalog and legacy is precisely the kind of subject that No Dogs in Space has built its audience around.

The podcast's first two seasons appear to have developed a dedicated following among listeners who appreciated the show's exhaustive approach to music history. The shift toward experimental rock in season three, culminating in the Can series, seemed a natural fit for the show's editorial mission. However, the abbreviated nature of this season and the subsequent silence suggests production challenges or changing circumstances that have interrupted the podcast's release schedule.

For podcasts operating outside traditional media infrastructure, sustaining consistent output while maintaining research quality presents an ongoing challenge. No Dogs in Space's hiatus underscores the vulnerability of independent creative projects that depend on listener support and creator availability. The pause leaves unanswered whether the show intends to return with new seasons or whether the Can series represents its final examination of a band that defined experimental rock.
