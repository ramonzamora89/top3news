Xbox prices are increasing by up to €200 or £170

What's happening

Microsoft is raising Xbox console prices across Europe and the UK by significant amounts, with increases of up to €200 or £170 depending on the model. The price hikes follow an earlier round of US price increases announced in June and represent a major jump for consumers in these regions.

Who's involved

Microsoft Corporation, the American multinational technology company headquartered in Redmond, Washington, is implementing the price increases for its Xbox Series X and Series S consoles. These fourth-generation Xbox consoles, released in November 2020 as part of the ninth generation of gaming hardware, are the focus of the pricing adjustment.

Why it matters

The dramatic price increases may significantly impact consumer purchasing decisions in Europe and the UK, potentially affecting Xbox's competitive position against Sony's PlayStation 5 in these key markets. For consumers already facing economic pressures, these hikes represent a substantial barrier to entry for next-generation gaming hardware.

The story

Microsoft is implementing substantial price increases for its Xbox console lineup in Europe and the United Kingdom, marking a significant shift in pricing strategy for the company's gaming division. The price bumps, which can reach as high as €200 or £170 depending on the specific model, were not immediately disclosed when Microsoft announced its June price increases, which at that time only included US pricing details.

The Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S, which launched in November 2020 as the company's fourth-generation console offering, are both subject to these regional price adjustments. The Series X, Microsoft's higher-end model that includes disc drive capability in its 1TB variant, appears to be among the most significantly affected by the increases. According to The Verge, the pricing adjustments represent one of the largest regional price hikes the company has implemented for its current-generation hardware.

These price increases come as Microsoft continues to navigate a challenging market environment for gaming hardware. The company's Xbox consoles compete directly with Sony's PlayStation 5, which was also released in November 2020 as part of the same ninth generation of gaming hardware. Both manufacturers have previously adjusted pricing in response to various economic factors, though the magnitude of Microsoft's European and UK increases appears particularly steep.

The timing of the price announcement, following an initial disclosure of US pricing changes in June, suggests Microsoft may have been deliberate in staggering its regional pricing communications. By delaying the release of European and UK pricing details, the company may have sought to manage the overall impact of its pricing strategy announcements across different markets.

For consumers in Europe and the UK, the increases represent a considerable additional investment in next-generation gaming technology. The 1TB Xbox Series X with a disc drive, which appears to be one of the most affected SKUs, now carries a substantially higher price barrier than before, potentially influencing purchasing decisions among budget-conscious gamers in these regions.
