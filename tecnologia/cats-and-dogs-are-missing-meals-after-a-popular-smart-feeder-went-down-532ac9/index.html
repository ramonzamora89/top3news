Cats and dogs are missing meals after a popular smart feeder went down

What's happening

Petlibro's smart pet feeding system has gone offline, leaving pet owners unable to dispense scheduled meals to their cats and dogs. The outage began Tuesday and has affected multiple Petlibro devices, including smart feeders and litter boxes, according to reports from frustrated users.

Who's involved

Petlibro is a company that manufactures connected pet care devices, including the Granary 2 smart feeder and other IoT-enabled pet products. The outage has impacted pet owners who rely on these devices for automated feeding and pet care management.

Why it matters

The outage exposes the vulnerability of pet care infrastructure that depends on cloud connectivity and company servers. When these systems fail, animals may miss scheduled meals, raising questions about backup mechanisms and the risks of outsourcing critical pet care functions to connected devices.

The story

Petlibro's smart pet feeding ecosystem went offline on Tuesday, disrupting the daily care routines of pet owners who depend on the company's connected devices to dispense meals and manage other tasks. Users across Reddit began reporting that their smart feeders and litter boxes were no longer responding to scheduled commands, leaving their cats and dogs without automatically-dispensed food. The Petlibro Granary 2, one of the company's flagship products, was among the devices affected by the outage.

The outage highlights a growing trend in pet care technology, where connected devices and cloud-based systems handle routine tasks that were once entirely manual. Smart feeders like Petlibro's offerings allow owners to schedule meals, monitor consumption, and control feeding remotely through smartphone apps. These conveniences come with a significant trade-off: when the company's servers go offline, the devices lose core functionality, even if they remain physically present in a pet's home.

According to The Verge, the scope of the outage extends beyond just feeders. Petlibro's product line includes smart litter boxes and other connected pet devices, all of which appear to have been affected when the company's backend systems went down. Pet owners relying on these devices for daily care suddenly had no way to ensure their animals received scheduled meals or had access to clean litter facilities without manual intervention.

For many pet owners, the outage created an urgent practical problem. Those who were at work or away from home when the outage occurred had no way to feed their pets remotely and faced the prospect of animals going hungry until they could manually refill feeders or return home. The incident underscores the dependency that users develop on these systems and the potential consequences when companies providing critical infrastructure experience service disruptions.

The outage raises important questions about device design and fallback mechanisms. Ideally, smart pet feeders would include local backup features that allow scheduled feeding to continue even when cloud connectivity is lost. However, many connected devices prioritize cloud-dependent features over offline functionality, leaving pets vulnerable when internet connectivity or company servers fail.

Petlibro has not yet publicly detailed the cause of the outage or provided a timeline for restoration. As pet owners await service restoration, the incident serves as a reminder of the risks inherent in delegating essential pet care tasks to connected devices that depend on external infrastructure beyond user control.
