Phil Collins Says He Almost Died in 2024 From Alcohol Abuse: ‘People Were Coming to Say Goodbye’

What's happening

Phil Collins has revealed that he nearly died in 2024 from health complications related to alcohol abuse, disclosing that his condition became so grave that people visited to say farewell. The Genesis drummer and solo artist was initially admitted to a Swiss hospital for alcohol addiction in late 2023.

Who's involved

Phil Collins is the English drummer and former lead vocalist of the rock band Genesis, one of the most commercially successful rock acts of the 1970s and beyond. He also had a highly successful solo career, achieving multiple chart-topping singles between 1982 and 1990.

Why it matters

The disclosure highlights the severe health risks of untreated alcohol addiction and the precarious state of Collins's health during 2024, illustrating how quickly addiction can become life-threatening. For fans and the music industry, it underscores both the vulnerability of aging rock legends and the ongoing challenges prominent figures face with substance abuse recovery.

The story

Phil Collins, the legendary drummer and vocalist best known for his decades-long tenure with Genesis, has opened up about a harrowing brush with death in 2024 stemming from complications of alcohol abuse. In an interview with The Times of London, Collins revealed that his condition deteriorated so severely that visitors came to bid him goodbye, indicating how close he came to losing his life.

Collins was first admitted to a Swiss hospital in late 2023 for treatment of alcohol addiction. After an initial discharge, his health took a troubling turn in 2024 when complications from his addiction emerged. According to The Times, the situation became critical enough that those close to him prepared for the worst.

The Genesis frontman's struggle with addiction comes at a stage of his life when many thought he had already overcome such challenges. Collins achieved enormous success both as the drummer of Genesis—a band formed in 1967 that became a pioneer of progressive rock—and as a solo artist, amassing more US top-40 singles than virtually any other artist during his peak years in the 1980s and early 1990s. His prominence in rock music made his personal battles public in ways that lesser-known figures might avoid.

The revelation also underscores the particularly acute dangers facing musicians and public figures in recovery. Collins's case demonstrates that even after formal hospitalization and treatment, the path to sustained recovery from alcohol abuse can be unpredictable and medically complex, with life-threatening complications arising even months after initial intervention.

Collins's decision to speak publicly about his near-fatal experience may serve as a cautionary tale about the progressive nature of alcohol addiction and the importance of comprehensive, ongoing medical support. His survival and willingness to discuss the experience offer a rare glimpse into the private health crises that can unfold behind the scenes for aging rock icons.
