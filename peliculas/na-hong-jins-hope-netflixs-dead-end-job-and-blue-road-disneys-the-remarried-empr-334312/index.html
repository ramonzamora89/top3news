Na Hong-Jin’s ‘Hope,’ Netflix’s ‘Dead-End Job’ and ‘Blue Road,’ Disney+’s ‘The Remarried Empress’ Among Selections as Busan Unveils Korean Cinema Today, On Screen Lineups

What's happening

The 31st Busan International Film Festival has announced its Korean Cinema Today and On Screen selections, featuring Na Hong-Jin's thriller 'Hope' alongside eight other films across multiple categories. The lineups showcase a mix of high-profile releases and festival premieres from South Korean cinema.

Who's involved

Na Hong-Jin is a celebrated South Korean director known for violent thrillers and horror elements, with breakthrough successes including The Chaser (2008) and The Yellow Sea (2010). The Busan International Film Festival, held annually in Busan since 1996, is one of Asia's most significant film festivals and the first international film festival established in South Korea.

Why it matters

These selections highlight the current state of Korean cinema at a major Asian festival platform, with Na Hong-Jin's latest work drawing international attention ahead of theatrical release. Festival premieres and industry showcases like these influence global distribution deals, critical reception, and the trajectory of Korean films in international markets.

The story

The 31st Busan International Film Festival has unveiled its Korean Cinema Today and On Screen selections, cementing the event's role as a major showcase for domestic South Korean talent. Na Hong-Jin's 'Hope' headlines a nine-film lineup that spans both the Panorama and Special Premiere sections, drawing significant global attention even before its wider theatrical release. According to Variety, the festival's curation reflects the breadth and ambition of current Korean filmmaking across multiple genres and formats.

Na Hong-Jin has established himself as one of South Korea's most distinctive voices through a filmography defined by violent thrillers and horror elements. His 2008 feature debut, The Chaser, became a watershed moment for the director, achieving both critical acclaim and commercial success as the third-highest-grossing film in South Korea that year. The Yellow Sea followed in 2010, earning international recognition when it was selected for the Un Certain Regard section at the Cannes Film Festival, further solidifying Na's reputation as a filmmaker capable of commanding global stages.

Beyond Na's 'Hope,' the Korean Cinema Today and On Screen selections include Netflix's 'Dead-End Job' and 'Blue Road,' as well as Disney+'s 'The Remarked Empress,' signaling the growing influence of streaming platforms in shaping Korean cinema's international profile. This mix of theatrical releases and platform-exclusive content reflects broader shifts in how Korean films reach audiences globally and how festivals position themselves within an evolving media landscape.

The Busan International Film Festival, held annually in Haeundae District since its inaugural event in September 1996, has long served as a crucial venue for Korean cinema. As the first international film festival established in South Korea, it has consistently championed domestic filmmakers while providing a gateway for Korean works to reach international buyers, critics, and audiences. The festival's emphasis on Korean Cinema Today programming demonstrates its continued commitment to celebrating and elevating current homegrown talent.

The announcement of these selections underscores the festival's influence in shaping the year's conversation around Korean film exports and industry recognition. For filmmakers like Na Hong-Jin, a Busan premiere or special selection can significantly amplify a film's visibility among international distributors and press. The inclusion of major streaming titles alongside traditional theatrical releases also reflects how Korean cinema now operates across multiple exhibition platforms simultaneously.
