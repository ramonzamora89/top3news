Becoming Tate McRae: The Pop Sensation on Navigating Fame, Treating Work Like ‘a Fantasy’ and Getting Taylor Swift’s ‘Amazing’ Sourdough Bread

What's happening

Tate McRae is at the center of a Variety profile exploring her rise as pop music's latest breakout star, her recent relocation to New York, and the personal and professional dynamics shaping her ascent. The piece offers insight into how the Canadian artist is navigating rapid fame while maintaining her creative vision.

Who's involved

Tate McRae is a Canadian singer, songwriter, and dancer who first gained prominence as a contestant on So You Think You Can Dance in 2016, then signed with RCA Records and achieved major streaming success with her 2021 EP Too Young to Be Sad, which became Spotify's most-streamed female EP that year. Taylor Swift is mentioned in the context of a personal connection, with McRae referencing Swift's sourdough bread.

Why it matters

The profile underscores McRae's consolidation as a major pop force at a moment when her career trajectory is accelerating—she's moved coasts, built a substantial fanbase, and is being positioned alongside established superstars in industry discourse. Her approach to fame and creative work offers a glimpse into how the current generation of pop artists are managing celebrity and artistry in a streaming-dominated landscape.

The story

Tate McRae celebrated her recent relocation to New York in early June by immersing herself in Knicks fever, marking a significant shift in her life less than a year after moving from Los Angeles. The move itself signals McRae's expanding sphere of influence: she arrived in L.A. as an emerging artist and is leaving as pop's next defining figure, now planting roots in another cultural epicenter. According to Variety, the piece explores not just the logistics of her geographic transition but the emotional and creative landscape she inhabits as her star continues to rise.

McRae's path to this moment has been deliberate and sustained. The Canadian artist first captured public attention as a contestant on So You Think You Can Dance in 2016, introducing her to millions as a multifaceted performer. Her early EPs, All the Things I Never Said (2020) and Too Young to Be Sad (2021), established her as a serious songwriter and recording artist. The latter project proved transformative: it became Spotify's most-streamed female EP of 2021 and was anchored by the single "You Broke Me First," which marked her breakthrough on the US Billboard Hot 100. That combination of streaming dominance and chart legitimacy positioned her within the upper echelon of contemporary pop.

What distinguishes McRae in the current pop landscape is her apparent ability to treat her work as creative play rather than mere commerce. The Variety profile suggests she approaches her career "like a fantasy," a framing that speaks to how she's maintaining creative agency amid the machinery of major-label success. This sensibility may reflect her background as a dancer—a discipline that prizes imagination and embodiment alongside technical precision.

The article also touches on McRae's intersection with established pop royalty, specifically Taylor Swift. A reference to receiving Swift's sourdough bread hints at a collegial acknowledgment between artists at different points in their careers. Swift, the highest-grossing live music artist and one of the best-selling musicians of all time, represents both a template for longevity and a reminder of the expectations now affixed to rising pop stars.

For McRae, the New York move appears emblematic of a broader confidence in her staying power. The city's cultural volatility and competitive intensity suggest she's positioning herself not as a temporary phenomenon but as an artist with long-term ambitions. Her ability to balance the demands of fame—the relocation, the profile coverage, the industry attention—while preserving her creative perspective will likely determine whether her current momentum translates into the kind of enduring career to which few pop artists aspire.
