Rubaiyat Hossain on Bodies, Beauty and Venice, Toronto and London-Bound ‘The Difficult Bride,’ Clip Unveiled (EXCLUSIVE)

What's happening

Bangladeshi filmmaker Rubaiyat Hossain is unveiling her fourth feature film, 'The Difficult Bride,' which has been selected for the Venice Film Festival and will also screen at Toronto and London. An exclusive clip from the film has been released, with Hossain discussing the project's origins and themes.

Who's involved

Rubaiyat Hossain is a Bangladeshi film director, writer, and producer whose previous works include 'Meherjaan' (2011), 'Under Construction' (2015), and 'Made in Bangladesh' (2019), the last of which won the Bangladesh National Film Award for Best Director in 2023 and was featured in the Criterion Collection. The Venice Film Festival, where 'The Difficult Bride' is being presented, is the world's oldest film festival and one of the five most prestigious international film festivals globally.

Why it matters

'The Difficult Bride' marks a significant milestone as the first Bangladeshi film selected for Venice's Orizzonti section, amplifying global recognition for Bangladeshi cinema on the world's oldest festival stage. Hossain's continued trajectory—from award-winning national recognition to major international film festival circuits—demonstrates growing international appetite for contemporary Bangladeshi storytelling and positions her as a key voice in global cinema.

The story

Rubaiyat Hossain's latest film, 'The Difficult Bride,' is generating significant anticipation ahead of its premiere on the festival circuit. According to Variety, the film will debut at the Venice Film Festival and is bound for screenings in Toronto and London, with an exclusive clip now unveiled. This marks a landmark moment for Bangladeshi cinema, as 'The Difficult Bride' becomes the first film from Bangladesh selected for the Orizzonti section of Venice—the world's oldest film festival and one of the five most prestigious international film festivals worldwide.

The genesis of 'The Difficult Bride' is deeply personal, rooted in a formative childhood memory. Hossain has explained that the film's central idea traces back to a story she heard at age seven while visiting a beauty parlor in Dhaka with her mother. A bride had died there—a story that circulated through her generation and left an indelible mark on the young filmmaker. That haunting encounter has evolved into the thematic and emotional core of her new work, which examines bodies, beauty, and the complex social forces that shape women's lives.

Hossain arrives at this moment as an increasingly recognized voice in contemporary cinema. Her previous feature, 'Made in Bangladesh' (2019), earned her the Bangladesh National Film Award for Best Director in 2023, making her the second female director to achieve this recognition in the country. That same film was recognized by the Criterion Collection as a hidden gem of the 2010s, signaling international critical validation of her artistic vision and storytelling depth.

The selection of 'The Difficult Bride' for Venice represents both a personal achievement for Hossain and a broader recognition of Bangladeshi cinema on the global stage. By bringing her work to the festival circuit—Venice, Toronto, and London—Hossain's film will reach audiences and industry figures across multiple continents, potentially opening doors for greater international distribution and audience engagement with contemporary Bangladeshi filmmaking.

Through her body of work, Hossain has established herself as a director invested in examining social realities, particularly as they intersect with women's experiences. 'The Difficult Bride' appears to continue this trajectory, using the intimate setting of beauty and bodily transformation to explore larger questions about identity, tradition, and agency. The exclusive clip now available offers early audiences a glimpse into how Hossain has translated her childhood memory into cinematic form.
