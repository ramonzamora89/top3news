TV Academy Hall of Fame: Sheila Nevins, Absent Due to COVID, Thanks Dr. Fauci for the Vaccine; Bill Hader Pays Tribute to the ‘South Park’ Creators With a ‘Fish Dick’ Story and More

What's happening

The Television Academy Hall of Fame inducted new members Thursday night, but documentary legend Sheila Nevins was forced to miss the ceremony after testing positive for COVID-19. Despite her absence, Nevins sent remarks thanking Dr. Anthony Fauci for vaccine development, while fellow honoree Bill Hader paid tribute to the South Park creators with a memorable anecdote.

Who's involved

Sheila Nevins, the former president of HBO Documentary Films and holder of 31 individual Primetime Emmy Awards—more than any other person—was among those recognized. Bill Hader, the actor and comedian, also attended to celebrate the South Park creative team. The Television Academy hosted the event, which marks Nevins' latest honor in a career spanning over 1,000 documentary productions.

Why it matters

Nevins' COVID diagnosis underscores ongoing health risks even among vaccinated individuals and highlights her public advocacy for vaccination in her Hall of Fame remarks. Her induction recognizes a transformative career in documentary filmmaking that shaped the medium at one of television's most prestigious platforms. The ceremony itself celebrates artists who have fundamentally influenced the television industry.

The story

The Television Academy's Hall of Fame event Thursday night celebrated some of the medium's most influential figures, though one of the evening's honorees was notably absent. Sheila Nevins, the legendary documentary producer and former president of HBO Documentary Films, had to skip the in-person ceremony after testing positive for COVID-19. Despite her absence, Nevins made her presence felt through remarks thanking Dr. Anthony Fauci for his work developing the vaccine, an acknowledgment that punctuated an evening otherwise devoted to celebrating career achievements and creative legacies.

Nevins holds an unparalleled record in the television industry: 31 individual Primetime Emmy Awards, more than any other person in history. Her Emmy haul reflects a career of extraordinary prolific output—she oversaw the production of more than 1,000 documentary films during her tenure at HBO Documentary Films, establishing the division as a powerhouse for serious nonfiction storytelling. Beyond Emmys, her work has been recognized with 42 Peabody Awards and 26 Academy Awards, cementing her influence across the industry's most prestigious honors. According to Variety, Nevins' videotaped remarks to the Hall of Fame ceremony demonstrated her commitment to public health messaging even while sidelined by illness.

The ceremony also featured tributes to other honorees. Bill Hader took the stage to celebrate the creators of South Park, sharing an anecdote involving the phrase "fish dick"—a reference to one of the animated series' most infamous running gags. His remarks highlighted the irreverent comedic legacy the South Park team has built over decades of provocative and inventive television.

Nevins' forced absence from the event, despite her vaccination status, serves as a reminder that COVID-19 continues to affect public figures and events even as the nation has largely moved past acute pandemic restrictions. Her decision to publicly credit vaccine development in her remarks, even while unable to attend in person, underscored the dual narrative: vaccines have been crucial in preventing severe illness, yet breakthrough infections remain possible. The juxtaposition of her absence and her gratitude toward vaccine development added an unscripted poignancy to an evening meant to honor television's most enduring creative figures.
