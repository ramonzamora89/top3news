Trisha Krishnan Toplines Panorama Studios’ First Tamil Film, a Psychological Horror Shot in London (EXCLUSIVE)

What's happening

Panorama Studios is launching its first Tamil-language film, a psychological horror starring Indian actress Trisha Krishnan and directed by Raja Krishna Menon. Principal photography is underway in London with a yet-to-be-titled project.

Who's involved

Trisha Krishnan is a leading Indian actress known for two decades of work in Tamil and Telugu cinema, often referred to as the "Queen of South India" and among the country's highest-paid performers. Raja Krishna Menon is an Indian filmmaker who transitioned from advertising direction to feature films, having directed five Hindi productions to date.

Why it matters

The project marks Panorama Studios' entry into Tamil-language production, signaling potential expansion of the studio's portfolio into South Indian cinema. It also represents a notable collaboration between established Tamil cinema talent and a filmmaker known for Hindi film work, potentially bridging regional film industries.

The story

Panorama Studios is moving into Tamil-language cinema with a psychological horror film headlined by Trisha Krishnan, one of India's most bankable leading actresses. The project, which began principal photography in London, marks the studio's first venture into Tamil production and features Radhika Sarathkumar and Aparna Balamurali in supporting roles. The film remains untitled, though the psychological horror genre choice signals an experimental direction for the studio's expansion into South Indian markets.

Directing the film is Raja Krishna Menon, an Indian writer, director, and producer whose career trajectory began in advertising before moving to narrative features. According to Variety, Menon is helming this Tamil production, marking a notable collaboration between a filmmaker known primarily for Hindi cinema and the Tamil film industry's established star power.

Krishnan's involvement underscores her stature in Indian cinema. The actress has maintained a leading position in Tamil and Telugu productions for over two decades, earning numerous accolades including five Filmfare Awards South and a Tamil Nadu State Film Award. Her designation as the "Queen of South India" reflects her sustained commercial and critical success, making her a marquee draw for a studio's inaugural regional language production.

The decision to shoot in London rather than India or within Tamil Nadu adds an international dimension to the project, potentially expanding its appeal beyond regional audiences. Psychological horror remains a relatively underexplored genre in Tamil cinema compared to traditional family dramas and romantic narratives, suggesting the film may target a more niche or genre-conscious demographic.

The project represents a calculated bet by Panorama Studios on both the director's vision and Krishnan's bankability to justify investment in an untested property within the Tamil market. As production continues, the film's eventual release could signal whether larger studios view South Indian language productions as expansion opportunities worthy of significant resources and established industry talent.
