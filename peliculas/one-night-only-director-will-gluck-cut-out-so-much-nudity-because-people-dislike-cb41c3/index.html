‘One Night Only’ Director Will Gluck Cut Out ‘So Much Nudity’ Because People Dislike Sex on Screen: ‘It Takes You Out of the Movie’

What's happening

Director Will Gluck is defending his creative choices for the upcoming romantic comedy "One Night Only," revealing he has cut significant nudity from the film because audiences find explicit content distracting during movies. Gluck is pushing back against questions about the film's premise and logic.

Who's involved

Will Gluck is an American filmmaker known for directing romantic comedies and family films including "Easy A" (2010), "Friends with Benefits" (2011), and the "Peter Rabbit" franchise. He is currently working on "One Night Only," a raucous romantic comedy scheduled for 2026 release.

Why it matters

Gluck's editorial decisions reflect ongoing industry debates about how much sexual content audiences will tolerate in comedies before it becomes counterproductive to storytelling. His comments suggest filmmakers are actively recalibrating content strategy based on viewer feedback, which could influence how future romantic comedies approach intimacy scenes.

The story

Director Will Gluck is taking a deliberate approach to depicting intimacy in his upcoming film "One Night Only," a romantic comedy centered on a fictional world where premarital sex is legal for one night per year. According to Variety, Gluck has been fielding numerous questions about the film's premise, logic, and potential plot holes, but he is urging audiences not to overthink the concept.

Instead, Gluck has been transparent about one major creative decision: he has substantially reduced the amount of nudity in the film. His reasoning is straightforward—he believes that explicit sexual content, rather than enhancing the viewing experience, actually pulls audiences out of the story. The director views nudity as a distraction that undermines the emotional investment viewers have in the narrative and characters.

This choice reflects a broader recalibration within the comedy genre. Gluck, who has built a career on romantic and comedic storytelling with films like "Easy A" and "Friends with Benefits," appears to be responding to audience preferences that have emerged over recent years. By cutting back on nudity, Gluck is betting that the film's humor, romance, and character development will resonate more powerfully than explicit imagery.

The film's premise—a world where one night annually permits premarital sexual relations—is inherently provocative and ripe for comedic exploration. However, Gluck's restraint in depicting that premise suggests he believes the comedy can be sharper and the emotional arcs more resonant when the focus remains on character interaction rather than graphic depiction.

Gluck's comments come as "One Night Only" prepares for its 2026 release, positioning the film within an evolving landscape of how mainstream comedies approach adult themes. His willingness to discuss these editorial decisions publicly may help set audience expectations and signal that the film prioritizes story and humor over shock value.
