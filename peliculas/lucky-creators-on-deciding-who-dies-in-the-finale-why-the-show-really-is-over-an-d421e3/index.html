‘Lucky’ Creators on Deciding Who Dies in the Finale, Why the Show Really Is Over and Not Knowing About Drew Starkey’s ‘Avid’ Fandom ‘Until We Killed Him’

What's happening

The creators of Apple TV's "Lucky" are discussing major decisions made in the show's finale, including which characters die and why the series is definitively ending rather than continuing.

Who's involved

Anya Taylor-Joy stars as protagonist Luciana "Lucky" Armstrong in the Apple TV+ series. Apple TV+ is a subscription streaming service owned by Apple that launched in 2019 and offers original films and television series through Apple Studios.

Why it matters

The finale represents the end of a series on a major streaming platform, with creative decisions about character deaths and narrative closure reflecting how showrunners craft endings in the prestige television era. The interview reveals behind-the-scenes dynamics, including the creators' discovery of actor Drew Starkey's deep fandom for the show only after killing off his character.

The story

The creators of Apple TV's "Lucky" are opening up about the high-stakes decisions that shaped the series finale, "All Good Things," including the pivotal choice of which characters would not survive to the end. In the finale, the show's protagonist Luciana "Lucky" Armstrong, played by Anya Taylor-Joy, executes one final con involving her father, John, who has recently been released from incarceration. According to Variety, the showrunners discussed their reasoning behind the character deaths and confirmed that the series is truly ending rather than leaving the door open for future seasons.

Taylor-Joy, who rose to prominence following her breakthrough in the 2015 horror film "The Witch," has built a career navigating complex dramatic and genre material, from "Split" and "Glass" to appearances in the crime drama "Peaky Blinders." Her role as Lucky required her to anchor a series centered on elaborate schemes and moral ambiguity, making the character's final arc a crucial element of the show's conclusion.

One unexpected revelation to emerge from the creators' discussion was their discovery that actor Drew Starkey harbored deep affection for the series—a fact they apparently learned only after deciding to kill off his character. This gap between the creators' awareness and Starkey's actual investment in the material speaks to the sometimes-disconnected nature of behind-the-scenes creative decisions and actors' personal relationships with their roles.

The decision to definitively end "Lucky" rather than leave narrative threads for potential revival reflects a broader creative choice about how the story should conclude. The finale allows Taylor-Joy's character to complete her arc, pulling off one last con against a family member, which serves as both a character study and a thematic capstone to her journey throughout the series.

Apple TV+, which launched on November 1, 2019, has built its original programming slate through such limited series and concluded dramas, operating within the prestige television model that emphasizes storytelling completeness over extended runs. The platform's decision-making around series finales carries weight in an environment where subscribers expect meaningful conclusions to the stories they invest in.
