Tim Curry, ‘Rocky Horror Picture Show’ Star, Dies at 80

What's happening

Tim Curry, the iconic actor and singer best known for his role as Dr. Frank-N-Furter in The Rocky Horror Picture Show, has died at age 80 in Los Angeles. The British performer, who became a cultural phenomenon through the campy 1975 film and its stage predecessor, passed away on Tuesday.

Who's involved

Tim Curry was a British actor and singer who rose to prominence in the 1970s for his villainous and flamboyant roles on stage and screen. He earned an Emmy Award and received nominations for Grammy, Olivier, and Tony Awards throughout his career, becoming most famous for originating the role of Dr. Frank-N-Furter in both the 1973 stage musical and 1975 film adaptation of The Rocky Horror Show.

Why it matters

Curry's death marks the end of an era for one of theater and film's most distinctive performers, whose portrayal of Dr. Frank-N-Furter became an enduring touchstone in popular culture. The Rocky Horror Picture Show evolved into a cult classic with decades of midnight screenings and audience participation, cementing Curry's legacy as an influential figure who helped define a generation's approach to camp and gender-bending performance art.

The story

Tim Curry, the flamboyant British actor and singer who became a cultural icon through his portrayal of Dr. Frank-N-Furter in The Rocky Horror Picture Show, has died at 80 in Los Angeles, Variety reports. The cause of death was not immediately disclosed. Curry's death represents the loss of one of entertainment's most distinctive and versatile performers, whose career spanned decades of theatrical and film work.

Curry first achieved fame in the early 1970s when he originated the role of Dr. Frank-N-Furter in the stage musical The Rocky Horror Show, which premiered in 1973 and ran through 1975. His performance was nothing short of transformative—a flamboyant, sexually provocative portrayal that pushed theatrical boundaries and made him an overnight sensation. Two years after the stage show debuted, director Jim Sharman adapted the musical into a film, with Curry reprising his signature role in The Rocky Horror Picture Show (1975), which was produced by Lou Adler and Michael White and distributed by 20th Century Fox.

The Rocky Horror Picture Show, based on Richard O'Brien's original music, book, and lyrics, became far more than a mere film adaptation. It evolved into a genuine cultural phenomenon, spawning decades of midnight screenings in theaters across the country where audiences dressed in costume and participated in audience-participation traditions that became as legendary as the film itself. Curry's performance at the center of this spectacle—his commanding stage presence, his vocal ability, and his fearless commitment to a gender-nonconforming character—was the engine that made the entire enterprise work.

Beyond The Rocky Horror Picture Show, Curry built a formidable career as a performer known for his villainous and theatrical roles. He earned an Emmy Award and received nominations for a Grammy Award, two Olivier Awards, and three Tony Awards, reflecting the breadth of his work across theater, film, and television. His ability to inhabit complex, often unsettling characters made him a sought-after talent for decades, though it was always his early triumph as Dr. Frank-N-Furter that remained most visible to the general public.

Curry's legacy extends beyond his individual performances to his influence on how performers approach camp, theatrical expression, and the portrayal of characters who defy conventional categories. His work helped establish The Rocky Horror Picture Show as an enduring artifact of popular culture, one that continues to attract new generations of viewers and participants. The film's lasting appeal owes much to Curry's uncompromising performance and the cultural space he helped create for alternative and transgressive art.
