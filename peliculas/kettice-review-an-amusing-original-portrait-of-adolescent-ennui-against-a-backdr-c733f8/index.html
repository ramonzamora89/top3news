‘Ketticè’ Review: An Amusing, Original Portrait of Adolescent Ennui Against a Backdrop of Adult Cluelessness

What's happening

Variety reviews "Ketticè," a new film directed by Giovanni Tortorici that explores adolescent dissatisfaction and generational disconnect. The film stars Monica Bellucci in a role that showcases her distinctive talent for ironic, self-aware dramatic performance.

Who's involved

Monica Bellucci, an Italian actress with a long career spanning Italian, American, British, and French films, takes a lead role in "Ketticè." Director Giovanni Tortorici helms the project, crafting a portrait of teenage ennui set against parental incomprehension.

Why it matters

The film's fresh approach to adolescent storytelling—filtered through adult dysfunction—offers audiences a distinctive take on coming-of-age narratives. Bellucci's performance demonstrates the versatility of an accomplished actor whose particular gifts for irony and dramatic self-awareness have been underutilized by many directors.

The story

Monica Bellucci's distinctive on-screen presence has long been a calling card of her acting repertoire, yet according to Variety, few directors have fully capitalized on her most potent weapon: her sneer. In "Ketticè," director Giovanni Tortorici appears to understand precisely what to do with this talent. The film presents an amusing and original examination of adolescent ennui, viewed through the lens of adult cluelessness, with Bellucci given full creative rein to deploy her considerable sense of irony.

Bellucci's career trajectory—from fashion model to international film presence—has taken her through diverse genres and languages across Italian, American, British, and French productions. Yet her ability to project a self-aware drama queen sensibility, paired with genuine emotional depth, remains one of cinema's underutilized resources. When a director grants her the space to fully explore this dimension of her craft, the results are difficult to match.

"Ketticè" represents precisely the kind of project that allows Bellucci's particular strengths to flourish. The film's premise—positioning adolescent disenchantment against a backdrop of parental bewilderment—creates fertile ground for her brand of ironic observation. Rather than playing the scenario straight, she inhabits it with the kind of knowing distance that transforms a potentially familiar narrative into something fresh and engaging.

The film's approach to generational tension suggests that neither teenagers nor adults possess a monopoly on confusion or self-delusion. By centering Bellucci's performance—one that embraces irony rather than fighting it—Tortorici has crafted a work that acknowledges the comedy inherent in the human condition, particularly the gap between how adolescents perceive themselves and how adults perceive them.

For viewers seeking coming-of-age stories that avoid sentimentality while remaining fundamentally human, "Ketticè" offers something distinct. The film's success hinges significantly on Bellucci's willingness to embrace her natural instincts for dramatic irony, suggesting that perhaps the real question isn't whether she possesses these gifts, but rather why the directing community has been so slow to request them.
