Paul Thomas Anderson’s New Movie ‘Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall’ Sets World Premiere at NYFF

What's happening

Paul Thomas Anderson's concert film 'Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall' will premiere at the New York Film Festival on October 1st at Alice Tully Hall. The 35mm screening will feature the presence of both Anderson and Winter, the Geese frontman whose sold-out solo performance the film captures.

Who's involved

Paul Thomas Anderson is an acclaimed American filmmaker who has won three Academy Awards, three Golden Globe Awards, and four BAFTAs, and is the only director to have won the Academy Award for Best Director and major directorial prizes at Cannes, Venice, and Berlin film festivals. Cameron Winter is the frontman of the band Geese, whose sold-out solo concert is the subject of the film.

Why it matters

This premiere marks a notable expansion of Anderson's filmmaking into concert documentation, a relatively rare venture for a director of his stature. The screening at NYFF, one of North America's most prestigious film festivals, signals significant institutional recognition for both the film and Winter as an artist.

The story

Paul Thomas Anderson's latest film, 'Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall,' will make its world premiere at the New York Film Festival on October 1st, according to Variety. The screening will take place at Alice Tully Hall in 35mm format, with both Anderson and Winter expected to attend the premiere.

The concert film captures a sold-out solo performance by Cameron Winter, frontman of the Brooklyn-based band Geese. Carnegie Hall, located at 881 Seventh Avenue in Midtown Manhattan between 56th and 57th Streets, is one of the world's most prestigious venues for both classical and popular music. The venue, designed by architect William Burnet Tuthill and built by industrialist and philanthropist Andrew Carnegie, hosts approximately 250 performances each season and represents a significant milestone for any artist performing there.

Anderson's foray into concert filmmaking represents a distinct shift from his previous work. The filmmaker is widely regarded as one of the preeminent directors of his generation, having garnered numerous accolades throughout his career. His track record includes three Academy Awards and recognition at Europe's three major film festivals—Cannes, Venice, and Berlin—making him the only director to achieve this particular distinction.

The New York Film Festival provides a high-profile platform for the film's debut. As one of North America's leading film festivals, NYFF attracts international attention and serves as a launching pad for significant works across multiple genres. The decision to screen the film on 35mm underscores a commitment to theatrical presentation and suggests Anderson's intention for the work to be experienced in a traditional cinema setting.

Winter's selection as the subject of an Anderson-directed concert film speaks to the growing recognition of Geese within the music landscape. The band has built a devoted following, evidenced by the sold-out nature of the Carnegie Hall performance captured in the film. This project demonstrates how established filmmakers continue to explore new subjects and formats while maintaining their artistic vision.
