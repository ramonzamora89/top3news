Taylor Swift Song ‘August’ Removed From Trump TikTok Video

What's happening

Donald Trump's campaign posted a TikTok video featuring Taylor Swift's song "August" four days ago, but the audio has since been removed. The video's caption now reads sarcastically, suggesting Swift would be unhappy about the use of her music.

Who's involved

Taylor Swift is the highest-grossing live music artist and one of the best-selling musicians of all time, known for autobiographical songwriting and artistic reinventions. Donald Trump, the 47th president of the United States and a Republican, posted the video through his campaign's official TikTok account.

Why it matters

The removal raises questions about copyright permissions and the relationship between high-profile artists and political campaigns. Swift has not publicly commented on the use of her music, but the incident highlights ongoing tensions between musicians and political figures over unauthorized or controversial use of their work.

The story

A TikTok video posted to Donald Trump's campaign account has had Taylor Swift's song "August" stripped of its audio after drawing attention online. The video, which was posted four days prior, originally featured the track as part of a "Team Trump" clip, according to Variety. The now-silent video carries a caption with a sarcastic tone: "I'm sure @Taylor Swift is going to be super excited we used…"

The removal of the audio suggests potential concerns over the use of Swift's music without explicit permission or approval. Swift, the wealthiest female musician in the world and a dominant figure in popular culture, has long been protective of her work and has been vocal about artistic and intellectual property rights. The incident comes amid broader scrutiny over how political campaigns use licensed music and celebrities' work.

No statement has been released from Swift or her representatives regarding the video or its initial use of "August." The song, from her 2020 album "folklore," is one of Swift's most popular tracks and is deeply personal in nature, featuring autobiographical elements characteristic of her songwriting style. The choice to feature it in a political campaign video raises questions about whether the campaign sought proper licensing or permission before posting.

The sarcastic caption on the amended video suggests the campaign was aware that Swift might object to its use, yet the phrasing does little to clarify whether the removal was voluntary or the result of a cease-and-desist request. TikTok's automated copyright detection systems can flag unauthorized music use, though they do not always result in immediate removal.

This incident is not the first time Swift's work has become entangled in political contexts. The artist, who has been notably cautious about public political statements until recent years, has maintained tight control over how her music is associated with causes and campaigns. The removal of "August" from the Trump campaign's TikTok underscores the ongoing tension between political organizations seeking to leverage popular culture and artists' desire to protect their work from unauthorized or unwanted political association.
