Trump Endorses U.S. Tax Incentive to Keep Hollywood Production: ‘Let’s Get This Done!’

What's happening

President Trump has publicly endorsed a federal tax incentive for film and TV production, signaling his support for a bipartisan legislative push to boost Hollywood's competitiveness. In a Truth Social post, Trump committed to advancing the measure, responding to years of industry advocacy.

Who's involved

Donald Trump, the 47th president of the United States and a Republican who previously served as the 45th president from 2017 to 2021, is championing the proposal. Hollywood studios and entertainment unions have long sought federal tax incentives to retain domestic production work.

Why it matters

A federal tax incentive could redirect significant production spending back to the U.S., where it has been declining as studios shift work to states and countries offering more generous local incentives. Industry success on this front would represent a rare alignment between Trump and Hollywood's business interests, potentially setting a precedent for bipartisan deal-making on competitive economic policy.

The story

President Trump has thrown his weight behind a federal tax incentive for film and television production, endorsing efforts that Hollywood studios and unions have pursued for years. In a statement posted to Truth Social on Monday, Trump announced his intention to push for bipartisan legislation designed to strengthen the industry's position domestically. "I am going to suggest that…Let's Get This Done!" he wrote, according to Variety, signaling an unusually direct commitment to the entertainment sector's policy agenda.

The endorsement marks a significant moment for an industry that has watched production dollars migrate away from traditional U.S. bases toward states offering aggressive tax breaks and international locations with comparable or superior incentives. Without a federal framework to level the playing field, American studios have found themselves at a structural disadvantage when competing for projects. The proposed federal tax credit would serve as a counterweight to these localized incentive programs, making domestic production more economically viable for major film and television projects.

Trump's embrace of the proposal is noteworthy given his Republican credentials and traditional skepticism of tax incentives; his willingness to champion it suggests recognition of the industry's economic heft and employment base. The plan's bipartisan framing indicates an effort to build coalition support across both parties, a necessary step for any legislation to advance through Congress. Industry representatives have characterized such federal action as essential to stemming the long-term erosion of American production capacity.

The timing of the endorsement comes as the entertainment industry continues to recover from recent labor disputes and seeks policy interventions to stabilize its competitive position globally. Studios and unions alike have unified around the tax incentive proposal, viewing it as a pragmatic solution to structural economic challenges rather than a partisan issue. A federal tax credit would reduce the effective cost of production in the United States relative to competing jurisdictions, potentially reversing years of outbound migration of jobs and infrastructure investment.

With Trump's explicit backing and his stated intention to pursue bipartisan support, the legislative pathway for a federal film and television production tax incentive appears clearer than it has in previous congressional sessions. The next phase will depend on whether his administration can translate the endorsement into concrete legislative language and sustain momentum through the lawmaking process.
