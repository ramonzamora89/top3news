CBS Orders Comedy Pilot ‘Hilda! (In Lights!)’ About 70-Year-Old Woman Pursuing Broadway Dreams

What's happening

CBS has greenlit a pilot for the comedy series "Hilda! (In Lights!)," centered on a 70-year-old woman who relocates to New York City to chase Broadway stardom. Writer and executive producer Abbey Caldwell has penned three scripts for the single-camera project.

Who's involved

CBS Broadcasting Inc., an American commercial broadcast television network and flagship property of CBS Entertainment Group division of Paramount Skydash, is backing the pilot. Abbey Caldwell serves as writer and executive producer on the comedy.

Why it matters

The pilot order signals CBS's interest in age-diverse storytelling and comedies centered on unconventional protagonists pursuing dreams later in life. If greenlit to series, the show could represent a meaningful expansion of roles for older actors in primetime broadcast television.

The story

CBS has ordered a pilot for "Hilda! (In Lights!)," a new comedy centered on an elderly woman pursuing Broadway fame, according to Variety. The single-camera project comes from writer and executive producer Abbey Caldwell, who has already completed three scripts for the potential series. The premise follows a 70-year-old protagonist who moves to New York City with ambitions of making it on the Broadway stage.

CBS Broadcasting Inc., one of Paramount Skydash's three flagship subsidiaries alongside Paramount Pictures and MTV, continues to develop fresh programming across multiple genres and demographics. The network's pilot order for "Hilda! (In Lights!)" represents an effort to expand its comedy slate with stories centered on nontraditional characters and life stages.

The project taps into a cultural moment where creators are increasingly mining the experiences of older adults for entertainment. Rather than relegating senior characters to supporting roles or stereotypical narratives, "Hilda! (In Lights!)" positions an elderly woman as the driving force of the comedy—someone with agency, ambition, and a specific goal. The New York City setting provides a backdrop rich with potential for both fish-out-of-water humor and genuine drama.

Caldwell's involvement as both writer and executive producer gives her significant creative control over the material from inception. The fact that three scripts are already completed suggests the project has moved beyond initial concept development and into a more concrete production phase, improving the likelihood of the pilot moving forward.

Broadway itself has historically skewed toward younger performers, making a comedy centered on an older woman's aspirations in that world a potentially fresh angle. The single-camera format chosen for the series allows for more cinematic storytelling and character-driven moments than a traditional multi-camera sitcom setup would provide.
