Austrian Director Sandra Wollner’s ‘Everytime’ Wins Best Film at Sarajevo Film Festival

What's happening

Austrian director Sandra Wollner's film 'Everytime' has won the Heart of Sarajevo award for best narrative feature at the Sarajevo Film Festival. The win marks another major victory for the film, which previously won at Cannes' Un Certain Regard section.

Who's involved

Sandra Wollner is an Austrian film director and screenwriter whose previous feature credits include The Impossible Picture (2016) and The Trouble with Being Born (2020). The Sarajevo Film Festival is the premier and largest film festival in Southeastern Europe, founded in 1995 and held annually in August.

Why it matters

The award underscores 'Everytime' as a significant work in contemporary European cinema and strengthens Wollner's standing as a major directorial voice. For the Sarajevo Film Festival, hosting such acclaimed international films reinforces its position as a major venue for serious cinema in Southeastern Europe and attracts global attention to the region's cultural institutions.

The story

Sandra Wollner's 'Everytime' has claimed the Heart of Sarajevo award for best narrative feature film at the Sarajevo Film Festival, cementing its status as one of the standout films of the festival season. According to Variety, the Austrian director's third feature was the unanimous choice of the festival's jury. The win represents a continuation of the film's remarkable trajectory through the international festival circuit.

The film's victory at Sarajevo comes on the heels of its success at the Cannes Film Festival, where it won in the Un Certain Regard section—a competition track known for showcasing daring, original films that often challenge conventional storytelling. This dual recognition from two of Europe's most prestigious festivals signals that 'Everytime' has resonated with festival juries and critics seeking provocative, distinctive voices in contemporary cinema.

Wollner has built her reputation through a series of formally inventive and thematically ambitious films. Her feature directorial debut, The Impossible Picture (2016), introduced her distinctive sensibility, which she further developed in The Trouble with Being Born (2020), a film that also generated significant critical attention. With 'Everytime', her third feature, Wollner appears to have reached a new level of recognition within the international film community.

The Sarajevo Film Festival, founded in 1995 during the siege of Sarajevo in the Bosnian War, has evolved into the premier and largest film festival in Southeastern Europe and one of the largest on the continent. Held annually in August, it brings international and local celebrities to Sarajevo and showcases an extensive variety of feature and short films from around the world. By awarding its top narrative prize to 'Everytime', the festival affirms its commitment to championing innovative and artistically significant cinema from across Europe.

The jury's unanimous decision to honor Wollner's film reflects the strength of her work and suggests that 'Everytime' has made a compelling case for itself among the year's best international cinema. The film is now positioned as a strong contender for consideration at additional festival awards and industry recognition moving forward.
