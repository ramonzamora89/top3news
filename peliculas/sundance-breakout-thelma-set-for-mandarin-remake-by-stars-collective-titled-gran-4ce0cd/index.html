Sundance Breakout ‘Thelma’ Set for Mandarin Remake by Stars Collective, Titled ‘Grandma, Please’ (EXCLUSIVE)

What's happening

Stars Collective has acquired remake rights to "Thelma," the 2024 Sundance breakout hit, and is developing a Mandarin-language adaptation titled "Grandma, Please." The original film became Magnolia Pictures' highest-grossing narrative feature following its festival debut.

Who's involved

Stars Collective is pursuing the adaptation rights; Magnolia Pictures distributed the original "Thelma," which debuted at the Sundance Film Festival, the largest independent film festival in the United States. The original film's success at Sundance has positioned it as a valuable property for international adaptation.

Why it matters

The remake signals continued appetite for adapting successful indie films for international markets, particularly in Mandarin-language cinema. A successful "Grandma, Please" could expand "Thelma's" cultural reach and demonstrate the commercial viability of remaking recent Sundance discoveries for non-English-speaking audiences.

The story

Stars Collective has secured Mandarin-language remake rights to "Thelma," the breakout independent film from the 2024 Sundance Film Festival, and is developing an adaptation titled "Grandma, Please," according to Variety. The original film proved a major commercial success, becoming Magnolia Pictures' highest-grossing narrative feature following its festival premiere.

"Thelma" arrived at Sundance as an independent production but quickly gained significant traction, leading to its theatrical release through Magnolia Pictures. The film's performance at the box office demonstrated strong audience interest in the project, establishing it as one of the most commercially successful narratives to emerge from the festival in recent years. Sundance, organized by the Sundance Institute, remains the largest independent film festival in the United States, having drawn over 423,000 combined in-person and online viewers in 2023.

The decision by Stars Collective to develop a Mandarin-language remake reflects a growing trend of international producers acquiring rights to successful indie films for adaptation in other markets. By retitling the project "Grandma, Please," the production company appears to be emphasizing elements of the original narrative that may resonate with Mandarin-speaking audiences, though details about the adaptation's specific creative approach remain limited.

Magnolia Pictures, the independent distributor and production company that released the original film, maintains a track record of backing distinctive narratives that appeal to arthouse and crossover audiences. The studio's investment in "Thelma" paid significant dividends, with the film's strong performance validating both the original filmmakers' vision and the distributor's programming strategy.

The remake project represents an opportunity for Stars Collective to capitalize on the original film's critical and commercial momentum while introducing the story to a new geographic and linguistic market. As streaming platforms and international co-productions continue to reshape independent film distribution, acquiring and remaking proven successes has become an increasingly common strategy for production companies seeking to maximize a property's global potential.
