‘Heartstopper’ Star Rhea Norwood, ‘Enola Holmes’ Actor Susan Wokoma, ‘Dear England’ Writer James Graham Join U.K. National Youth Theatre’s 70th Anniversary Show (EXCLUSIVE)

What's happening

The U.K.'s National Youth Theatre has announced a fresh slate of celebrity participants for its 70th anniversary one-night performance, 'Letters to My Younger Future Self,' happening Sept. 20 at London's Southbank Centre. The cast includes Rhea Norwood from 'Heartstopper,' Susan Wokoma from 'Enola Holmes,' and playwright James Graham from 'Dear England.'

Who's involved

The National Youth Theatre is a London-based youth theatre and charity founded in 1956 as the world's first organization of its kind, dedicated to developing young people's artistic skills through theatrical productions. The organization has a storied track record of nurturing early talent, with alumni including Daniel Craig, Daniel Day-Lewis, Chiwetel Ejiofor, Colin Firth, and Ben Kingsley. Netflix is supporting the anniversary event.

Why it matters

The involvement of established television and stage actors in a youth theatre milestone underscores the organization's continued relevance as a talent incubator and its ability to attract high-profile industry figures. The Netflix partnership signals mainstream streaming platform interest in theatrical heritage and emerging talent development, potentially raising the profile of youth theatre programming beyond traditional theatrical audiences.

The story

The National Youth Theatre of Great Britain is marking seven decades of operation with a star-studded one-night celebration titled 'Letters to My Younger Future Self,' scheduled for Sept. 20 at the Southbank Centre's Royal venue. According to Variety, the cast announcement includes television and stage talent such as Rhea Norwood, known for her role in the popular 'Heartstopper' series; Susan Wokoma, recognized for 'Enola Holmes'; and James Graham, the acclaimed writer behind 'Dear England.'

The event carries particular symbolic weight given the National Youth Theatre's historic position in British theatre. Founded in 1956, the organization holds the distinction of being the world's first youth theatre, created with the explicit aim of developing young people's artistic skills through theatrical productions and other creative endeavours. Over nearly seven decades, it has established itself as a crucial launching pad for British talent, having nurtured the early careers of internationally recognized figures including Daniel Craig, Daniel Day-Lewis, Chiwetel Ejiofor, Colin Firth, and Ben Kingsley.

The decision to anchor the 70th anniversary celebration around the theme of letters to one's younger self suggests a reflective approach to marking the milestone—one that invites established performers to consider their own formative years in theatre. By casting current and former luminaries in a single performance, the organization creates a tangible link between generations of artists who have passed through its ranks.

Netflix's support of the event represents a notable intersection between streaming platforms and institutional theatre heritage. The partnership indicates growing interest from major media companies in theatrical celebrations and youth talent development, potentially expanding the reach of what might traditionally have been a London-based theatre event.

The announcement of additional cast members follows an earlier wave of participant confirmations, suggesting the organization is building momentum ahead of the September performance. As the National Youth Theatre approaches its anniversary, the event serves both as a celebration of institutional legacy and as a contemporary statement about the organization's continued role in shaping Britain's theatrical future.
