‘The Wrong Girls’ Review: Kristen Stewart and Alia Shawkat Are a Good Time in Dylan Meyer’s Shaggy Stoner Comedy

What's happening

Debut writer-director Dylan Meyer's stoner comedy "The Wrong Girls" has landed with strong reviews, pairing Kristen Stewart and Alia Shawkat in an absurdist adventure that echoes the stoner-comedy tradition while charting its own irreverent path.

Who's involved

Kristen Stewart, an American actress and filmmaker ranked among the world's highest-paid actors whose films have grossed over $4 billion globally, stars alongside Alia Shawkat, known for her roles in "Arrested Development" and "Search Party." Director Dylan Meyer is making her feature writing and directing debut with the film.

Why it matters

The film signals a fresh entry point for the stoner-comedy subgenre and demonstrates continued box-office appeal for Stewart in genre work beyond her established dramatic roles. Meyer's debut success could establish her as a notable new voice in comedy filmmaking.

The story

Kristen Stewart and Alia Shawkat are steering audiences through absurdist mayhem in Dylan Meyer's "The Wrong Girls," a debut feature that arrives as a spirited addition to the stoner-comedy canon. According to Variety, the film practically achieves liftoff with its shaggy, irreverent energy, suggesting Meyer has tapped into something that resonates beyond its niche premise.

The film positions itself within a lineage of cannabis-fueled comedies stretching back to 2001's "How High," which featured Method Man and Redman. Where that film offered one particular comedic sensibility, Meyer's picture presents a pair of mismatched protagonists navigating absurdist situations—though the summary suggests the specifics of their journey remain tantalizing rather than fully revealed in advance reviews.

Stewart, whose career has encompassed blockbusters and arthouse dramas alike, continues to expand her range into genre territory. With her films collectively grossing over $4 billion worldwide and accolades including a British Academy Film Award and a César Award, Stewart brings established credibility to what might otherwise be dismissed as a minor genre entry. Shawkat, meanwhile, brings her comedic timing honed across television roles in "Arrested Development," where she played Maeby Fünke, and the dark comedy series "Search Party," where she anchored the narrative as Dory Sief.

Meyer's ascent as a writer-director marks a notable moment in independent comedy production. As a debut feature, "The Wrong Girls" carries the weight of establishing her voice and vision—no small task in a landscape crowded with comedy projects. That critics have responded favorably suggests she has moved beyond proving ground into territory where her sensibility finds an audience.

The film arrives at a moment when stoner comedies, once a staple of mid-budget studio comedy, have largely migrated to streaming platforms or independent circuits. "The Wrong Girls" appears to be making a theatrical case for the form, banking on the star power and comedic chemistry of its leads to anchor what sounds like deliberately antic material.

Whether the film sustains its apparent early momentum through wider release will depend on word-of-mouth and the appetite for anarchic comedy among general audiences—terrain that has proven unpredictable in recent years.
