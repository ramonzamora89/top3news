Venice Chief Alberto Barbera Under Fire for Lack of Female Directors in Competition: ‘It Is a One-Dimensional Selection’

What's happening

Venice Film Festival artistic director Alberto Barbera is facing backlash for programming only one female-directed film in this year's 20-title competition lineup. Italian film organizations, including Women in Film Television and Media Italia, are calling the selection 'one-dimensional' and criticizing the sharp drop in female representation.

Who's involved

Alberto Barbera is the artistic director of the Venice Film Festival, one of the world's oldest and most prestigious film festivals. Women in Film Television and Media Italia is an advocacy organization pushing for gender equity in the Italian film industry.

Why it matters

The criticism highlights ongoing gender disparities in film festival programming and could pressure major festivals to implement more inclusive selection practices. This moment amplifies the broader conversation about women's representation in the film industry, where female directors remain significantly underrepresented in major competitions and funding opportunities.

More context

Film festivals have increasingly faced scrutiny over gender representation following years of advocacy by industry groups. Venice, Cannes, and Berlin have been under pressure to balance their lineups, with some festivals achieving more parity in recent years. The sharp drop in female-directed films at Venice this year—compared to previous selections—suggests a regression rather than progress, making the controversy particularly pointed in an industry already grappling with systemic barriers to female filmmakers' access and visibility.
