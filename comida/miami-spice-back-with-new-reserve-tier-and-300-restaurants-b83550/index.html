Miami Spice Back with New Reserve Tier and 300 Restaurants

What's happening

Miami Spice Restaurant Month is returning for its 25th edition, running August 1 through September 30, 2026. The program now features a new reserve tier and expanded participation from over 300 restaurants offering three-course prix-fixe menus with at least 30% savings.

Who's involved

The Greater Miami Convention & Visitors Bureau (GMCVB) is organizing the initiative. The GMCVB is Miami's official tourism promotion agency tasked with marketing the region to visitors and supporting the local hospitality and dining sectors.

Why it matters

The expansion of Miami Spice signals growing confidence in the region's restaurant scene and offers both residents and tourists significant savings during a two-month dining window. The addition of a reserve tier and increased restaurant count suggests the program has matured into a major driver of traffic and revenue for the city's hospitality sector.

The story

Miami's premier dining promotion is back. The Greater Miami Convention & Visitors Bureau has announced that Miami Spice Restaurant Month—the region's flagship culinary event—will run for two months this year, from August 1 through September 30, 2026. The 25th edition marks a significant expansion of the program, which now includes more than 300 participating restaurants and introduces a new reserve tier to the traditional offerings.

Miami Spice has long been a fixture on the local calendar, drawing both residents and visiting tourists eager to experience the city's diverse dining landscape at reduced prices. The core concept remains straightforward: participating restaurants offer three-course prix-fixe menus at a minimum of 30% savings off regular pricing. This year's expansion reflects the program's continued growth and the increasing appetite for curated dining experiences that deliver value without sacrificing quality.

The introduction of a reserve tier represents the most notable structural change to the program in recent memory. While the GMCVB has not detailed the specific mechanics of the reserve category, the addition signals an effort to tier the participating restaurants by level of prestige or demand, potentially allowing diners to book experiences at some of Miami's most sought-after establishments during the promotion period. Such a move could help balance interest across the full roster of participating venues and ensure that both casual eateries and high-end establishments benefit from the program's promotional power.

According to Miami Curated, the expansion to over 300 restaurants represents a substantial increase in participation compared to previous years, underscoring the program's appeal to the local dining community. The extended two-month window—longer than some previous iterations—also provides restaurants with additional time to capture diners' attention and allows consumers more flexibility in planning their culinary calendar.

The timing of Miami Spice in the late summer and early fall positions it strategically as the region transitions from its slower summer season into the busier fall and holiday periods. For restaurants, the program serves as an engine to drive covers during a traditionally quieter window. For diners, it offers an accessible entry point to sample restaurants they might not otherwise visit or to revisit established favorites at a discount.
