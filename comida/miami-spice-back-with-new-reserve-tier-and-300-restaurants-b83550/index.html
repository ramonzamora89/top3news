Miami Spice Back with New Reserve Tier and 300 Restaurants

What's happening

Miami Spice Restaurant Month is returning for its 25th edition, running from August 1 through September 30, 2026. The program now features a new reserve tier and has expanded to include more than 300 participating restaurants offering three-course prix-fixe menus at a minimum of 30% savings.

Who's involved

The Greater Miami Convention & Visitors Bureau (GMCVB) is organizing the event. The GMCVB is the official destination marketing organization for Miami-Dade County, responsible for promoting the region's tourism and hospitality industry.

Why it matters

Miami Spice is one of the region's largest annual dining promotions, driving significant foot traffic to restaurants and generating economic activity during the late summer season. The addition of a reserve tier and expanded restaurant count signals growing participation and sophistication in the program, potentially attracting both budget-conscious diners and upscale dining experiences.

The story

The Greater Miami Convention & Visitors Bureau has announced the return of Miami Spice Restaurant Month for its 25th consecutive year, establishing the program as a cornerstone event in the region's dining calendar. The two-month celebration will run from August 1 through September 30, 2026, featuring special prix-fixe menus at participating establishments throughout Miami-Dade County.

According to Miami Curated, this year's edition marks a notable expansion of the program, with more than 300 restaurants participating—a significant growth in participation. The program offers diners three-course prix-fixe menus at a minimum of 30% savings, making fine and casual dining more accessible during what is typically a slower period for hospitality businesses in South Florida. The addition of a new reserve tier to the program signals an effort to create tiered pricing options that appeal to a broader range of dining experiences and price points.

The history of Miami Spice reflects the city's evolution as a culinary destination. What began as a smaller promotional initiative has grown into one of the region's most anticipated dining events, attracting both locals and visitors seeking value-driven gastronomic experiences. The program's longevity—now spanning a quarter-century—demonstrates sustained commitment from the local hospitality industry and the convention bureau to maintain Miami's appeal during the summer months.

For restaurants, the event provides a crucial revenue opportunity during a traditionally quiet season in South Florida's hospitality sector. The exposure and foot traffic generated by the program often introduce diners to establishments they might not otherwise visit, potentially building long-term customer relationships beyond the promotional period.

The expansion to over 300 venues and the introduction of the reserve tier suggest that organizers are responding to both demand from consumers and interest from the restaurant community. The stratified approach allows high-end establishments to participate alongside casual dining options, broadening the appeal of the program and ensuring there are offerings for every budget level.
