Where Chic Meets Flavor: Septimo & Nuna at Four Seasons Miami

What's happening

Four Seasons Miami is spotlighting two of its bars—Septimo and Nuna—as premier destinations for upscale dining and drinking. The property is positioning these venues as exemplars of luxury hospitality that blend sophisticated design with culinary excellence.

Who's involved

Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts, a Canadian luxury hotel company headquartered in Toronto, operates the Miami property. The company is majority-owned by Bill Gates and Prince Al-Waleed bin Talal, with Gates-controlled Cascade Investment holding 71.25% and Kingdom Holding Company holding 23.75% as of January 2022.

Why it matters

The focus on curated bar experiences reflects how luxury hotels are differentiating themselves in an increasingly competitive hospitality market by emphasizing distinctive venues and service quality. For Miami's tourism and dining scene, Four Seasons' investment in standout bars signals continued elevation of the city's upscale leisure offerings.

The story

Four Seasons Miami is drawing attention to two signature bars—Septimo and Nuna—as defining elements of its luxury hospitality offering in South Florida. According to Miami Curated, the property exemplifies what constitutes a truly exceptional bar: one that combines chicness and elegance with refined service and thoughtful amenities that distinguish world-class establishments.

The bars operate within Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts, the Canadian luxury hotel and resort company headquartered in Toronto, Ontario. As of 2026, Four Seasons operates more than 130 hotels and resorts worldwide. Since 2007, the company has been majority-owned by Bill Gates and Prince Al-Waleed bin Talal, with Gates-controlled Cascade Investment owning 71.25% and Alwaleed-controlled Kingdom Holding Company owning 23.75% as of January 2022. This ownership structure underscores the company's commitment to premium, globally recognized hospitality standards.

Septimo and Nuna represent the kind of curated experiences that define luxury hotel bars in flagship markets. Rather than generic lounges, these venues are designed as destinations unto themselves, where attention to detail—from cocktail craftsmanship to interior ambiance—elevates the entire experience. The emphasis on "special touches" and amenities reflects how premium establishments compete not merely on location or brand name, but on the quality of every guest interaction and sensory detail.

For Miami's hospitality landscape, the prominence of these bars signals the ongoing importance of distinctive F&B venues as anchors for luxury hotel portfolios. The city has long competed for high-end tourism dollars, and Four Seasons' investment in signature bars demonstrates how properties maintain relevance by curating memorable experiences rather than relying solely on room inventory or standard resort amenities.

The focus on these two bars also underscores a broader shift in luxury hospitality: the recognition that bars and lounges are no longer afterthoughts but rather key destinations that attract locals and visitors alike. A truly exceptional bar combines the glamour and sophistication that define high-end travel with genuine hospitality—the kind of establishment where guests feel both welcomed and elevated.
