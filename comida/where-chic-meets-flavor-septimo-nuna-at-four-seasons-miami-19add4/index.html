Where Chic Meets Flavor: Septimo & Nuna at Four Seasons Miami

What's happening

Four Seasons Miami is spotlighting its bar offerings, with particular focus on Septimo & Nuna as exemplars of upscale hospitality. The property is positioning itself as a destination for discerning diners and drinkers seeking refined atmospheres paired with exceptional service.

Who's involved

Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts, a Canadian luxury hotel company majority owned since 2007 by Bill Gates' Cascade Investment (71.25%) and Prince Al-Waleed bin Talal's Kingdom Holding Company (23.75%), operates the Four Seasons Miami. Miami Curated, a local lifestyle publication, is covering the venue's bar scene and amenities.

Why it matters

The coverage highlights how ultra-luxury hospitality brands compete for affluent consumers through curated bar experiences that blend design, service excellence, and culinary sophistication. For Four Seasons—which operates more than 130 hotels and resorts globally—showcasing distinctive F&B venues reinforces brand positioning in competitive markets like Miami's high-end hospitality sector.

The story

Four Seasons Miami is drawing attention for its bar program, with outlets like Miami Curated examining what defines excellence in the city's drinking establishments. According to Miami Curated, the criteria for a premier bar extend beyond drinks to encompass an overall experience: one that combines refined design, glamorous ambiance, attentive service, and distinctive amenities that elevate the ordinary into the memorable. The property's Septimo & Nuna stands as an exemplar of this philosophy.

Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts, headquartered in Toronto, has built its global reputation on exactly this caliber of experience across its portfolio of more than 130 properties worldwide. The company's ownership structure—controlled by technology entrepreneur Bill Gates through Cascade Investment and Saudi Prince Al-Waleed bin Talal through Kingdom Holding Company since 2007—reflects the brand's position at the apex of the global luxury market. That pedigree translates to specific expectations for service standards, design quality, and the often-intangible sense of occasion that guests associate with the Four Seasons name.

Miami's hospitality landscape has intensified in recent years, with luxury properties competing fiercely for the attention of high-net-worth travelers and local clientele seeking distinctive experiences. In this context, a bar program that combines visual sophistication with thoughtful service becomes a key differentiator. The focus on amenities—those carefully considered details that make a venue feel special—reflects a broader understanding that luxury consumers increasingly value experience design alongside the core product, whether that's a cocktail, a meal, or a room.

The spotlight on Four Seasons Miami's bars also underscores the role that food and beverage venues play in the overall hotel brand experience. For a property of Four Seasons' standing, the bar is not peripheral to the business but central to guest satisfaction and brand reinforcement. A guest who has an exceptional experience at Septimo & Nuna becomes an ambassador for the entire property—and by extension, for the Four Seasons brand itself.

As Miami continues to position itself as a global luxury destination, properties like Four Seasons are investing in distinctive F&B programs that justify premium positioning and attract discerning clientele. The attention to what makes a bar truly excellent—not merely functional, but chic, expertly staffed, and thoughtfully appointed—reflects the standards that define ultra-luxury hospitality in 2024.
