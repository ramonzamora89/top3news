Where Chic Meets Flavor: Septimo & Nuna at Four Seasons Miami

What's happening

Four Seasons Miami is spotlighting two of its premier bars—Septimo and Nuna—as destinations for sophisticated cocktails and upscale dining. The property is positioning these venues as exemplars of luxury hospitality that combine elegant design with culinary excellence.

Who's involved

Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts, a Canadian luxury hotel company headquartered in Toronto, operates the Miami property. The company, majority-owned since 2007 by Bill Gates and Prince Al-Waleed bin Talal through their respective investment vehicles, manages over 130 hotels and resorts globally.

Why it matters

The curation of high-end bar experiences reflects how luxury hospitality brands compete for affluent travelers seeking distinctive venues beyond traditional accommodations. As Four Seasons continues to expand its portfolio—now exceeding 130 properties worldwide—differentiated amenities and signature dining concepts have become central to brand positioning and guest retention.

The story

Four Seasons Miami is drawing attention to Septimo and Nuna, two bars that exemplify the brand's commitment to merging sophisticated design with elevated service. According to Miami Curated, a bar earns its "best" designation through a combination of factors: an unmistakably chic aesthetic, polished service standards, and what the publication describes as special amenities that distinguish it from ordinary establishments. These are the kinds of venues typically found in the world's most prestigious luxury hotels.

Septimo and Nuna represent Four Seasons Miami's approach to hospitality beyond the guest room. The bars are designed as destinations in their own right, attracting both hotel guests and local patrons seeking an experience rooted in glamour and refinement. The specifics of each venue's menu, design language, and signature offerings underscore how contemporary luxury hotels function as lifestyle destinations rather than mere places to sleep.

Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts, headquartered in Toronto, has built its reputation on precisely this kind of comprehensive luxury experience. The company operates more than 130 hotels and resorts worldwide, with majority ownership held since 2007 by Bill Gates through Cascade Investment (71.25%) and Prince Al-Waleed bin Talal through Kingdom Holding Company (23.75%). This ownership structure has enabled significant investment in properties and their amenities across diverse markets.

The Miami market in particular has become increasingly competitive for luxury hospitality. The city attracts a clientele with sophisticated tastes and high expectations for service, design, and culinary innovation. By emphasizing distinctive bar concepts like Septimo and Nuna, Four Seasons positions itself not merely as accommodation but as a curator of memorable experiences. These venues serve as extensions of the brand's identity and contribute meaningfully to the overall guest journey.

The emphasis on specialized bar programming and design reflects a broader shift in how luxury hotel brands differentiate themselves. Rather than relying solely on room quality and standard amenities, properties now invest heavily in signature dining and drinking venues that generate their own reputation and draw independent visitors. For Four Seasons Miami, Septimo and Nuna represent this evolution—spaces where elegance and flavor converge to create the kind of refined environment that discerning travelers actively seek.
