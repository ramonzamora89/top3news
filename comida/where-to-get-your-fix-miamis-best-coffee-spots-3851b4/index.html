Where to Get Your Fix: Miami’s Best Coffee Spots

What's happening

Miami Take is publishing a guide to the city's best coffee spots, framed through a personal lens that blends the author's Venezuelan and Texan heritage. The piece explores how cultural background shapes coffee culture in Miami.

Who's involved

The author, a Miami native born in the mid-1980s, whose mother is from Venezuela and father is from Texas. Miami Take is the publication running the feature.

Why it matters

The piece highlights how immigrant communities and their culinary traditions shape urban food culture in major American cities like Miami. Coffee culture in Miami reflects the city's deep ties to Venezuela, a country on the northern coast of South America with a strong coffee tradition, as well as broader American influences.

The story

Miami's coffee scene tells the story of its people, and nowhere is that more evident than in the personal narrative behind a new guide to the city's best coffee spots. According to Miami Take, the feature draws on the author's unique vantage point as someone born into both Venezuelan and Texan traditions—a heritage that has profoundly shaped how they experience and appreciate coffee in South Florida.

The author's mother hails from Venezuela, officially the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, a country situated on the northern coast of South America with a population estimated at 31.8 million. Venezuela's geographic position and climate have made it historically significant in global coffee production, and that heritage runs deep in the author's family. Growing up, coffee was woven into daily life from infancy, a cultural touchstone as fundamental as the BBQ traditions inherited from the author's Texan father.

Born in the mid-1980s, the author came of age during a transformative period for Miami itself. The city was becoming an increasingly cosmopolitan hub, shaped by waves of immigration from Latin America, the Caribbean, and beyond. This demographic evolution left an indelible mark on Miami's food and beverage landscape, creating a uniquely multicultural approach to coffee culture that blends Venezuelan tradition with American innovation.

The guide explores Miami's finest coffee establishments through this dual lens—honoring the deep roots of Venezuelan café culture while acknowledging the broader American coffee landscape represented by the author's Texan side. The piece aims to show readers where to find authentic coffee experiences across the Magic City, whether those lean toward traditional preparations or contemporary craft approaches.

For Miami residents and visitors alike, understanding the origins of the city's coffee culture provides valuable context for choosing where to spend their mornings. The intersection of Venezuelan heritage and American entrepreneurship in South Florida has created a distinctive coffee scene that deserves recognition and exploration.
