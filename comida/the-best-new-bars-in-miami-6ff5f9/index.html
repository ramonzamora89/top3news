The Best New Bars in Miami

What's happening

Miami's bar scene is experiencing a notable shift toward vermouth and sherry-based cocktails, alongside classic Spanish drinks that reflect the city's position as a cultural crossroads. New establishments are capitalizing on Miami's long tradition of blending Caribbean, Latin American, and U.S. influences through elevated spirits and sophisticated flavor profiles.

Who's involved

Eater Miami, the food and drink publication covering South Florida's hospitality scene, is documenting the trend. The bars themselves represent Miami's broader hospitality industry, which serves a metropolitan area of approximately 6.39 million residents in South Florida.

Why it matters

The emergence of vermouth and sherry-focused bars signals a maturation of Miami's cocktail culture beyond its historical association with rum and agave spirits. This shift reflects both consumer demand for European-influenced craft cocktails and Miami's enduring identity as a gateway between three distinct drinking traditions, positioning the city as a destination for sophisticated spirits tourism.

The story

Miami's cocktail landscape is undergoing a subtle but significant transformation. According to Eater Miami, new bars throughout the city are increasingly turning to vermouth and sherry-based drinks, alongside classic Spanish cocktails, representing a departure from the rum and agave spirits that have long dominated the local scene. This shift reflects both the evolution of Miami's drinking culture and the city's unique position as a cultural intermediary.

Miami's geography and history have long made it a champion of diverse flavors. Positioned as a gateway between the United States, the Caribbean, and Latin America, the city has naturally become a hub where different spirits traditions converge. Rum from Caribbean producers and agave spirits from Mexico have historically mingled with locally popular citrus and exotic fruit flavors, creating a distinctive Miami cocktail identity. Now, European fortified wines are entering that equation.

Vermouth, an Italian aromatized fortified wine flavored with various botanicals, has a history stretching back to the mid-to-late 18th century in Turin, Italy. Sherry, its Spanish counterpart, carries centuries of tradition from Andalusia. These spirits, once considered strictly classical or old-fashioned, are experiencing a global renaissance among craft bartenders and cocktail enthusiasts seeking complexity and historical authenticity. Miami's new bars are tapping into this momentum while honoring the city's role as a meeting place of cultures.

The trend speaks to Miami's maturation as a cocktail destination. With a metropolitan population of 6.39 million residents and the third-largest skyline in the United States, the city has the scale and sophistication to support specialized drinking establishments. The arrival of vermouth and sherry-focused bars suggests that Miami's drinking audience is increasingly cosmopolitan, interested in exploring the full spectrum of global spirits culture rather than defaulting to regional specialties.

These new establishments are not simply transplanting European bar culture to South Florida; they are integrating vermouth and sherry into Miami's existing ecosystem of Caribbean and Latin American flavors. In doing so, they expand rather than replace the city's cocktail vocabulary, allowing bartenders to create drinks that reflect Miami's identity as a place where multiple traditions coexist and influence one another.
