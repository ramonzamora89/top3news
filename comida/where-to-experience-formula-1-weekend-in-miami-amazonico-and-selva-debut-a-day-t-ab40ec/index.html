Where to Experience Formula 1 Weekend in Miami: Amazónico and Selva Debut a Day-to-Night Race Destination

What's happening

Miami venues Amazónico and Selva are launching a coordinated day-to-night Formula 1 experience during Grand Prix weekend, featuring a race day brunch, rooftop gathering, and viewing party in the Brickell neighborhood.

Who's involved

Amazónico and Selva are hospitality and dining venues in Miami's Brickell district capitalizing on the Miami Grand Prix, which debuted in 2022 as part of the Formula One World Championship circuit.

Why it matters

The partnership reflects how major sporting events drive hospitality innovation and spending in host cities beyond the racetrack itself. For Miami's tourism and restaurant sectors, F1 weekends represent high-value visitor days that venues are now positioning to capture through extended programming rather than single-event attendance.

The story

Miami's hospitality scene is expanding its footprint during Formula 1 Grand Prix weekend with a new day-to-night racing experience. Amazónico and Selva, two prominent Brickell venues, are debuting a coordinated package that begins with a race day brunch, transitions into a rooftop gathering, and culminates in a Grand Prix viewing party. The strategy reflects a broader shift among Miami restaurants and bars to maximize the economic impact of major sporting events by offering immersive, multi-stage experiences rather than standalone dining.

The Miami Grand Prix itself is a relatively recent addition to the Formula One calendar. The event first took place during the 2022 season at the Miami International Autodrome, located on the grounds of Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens, a suburb situated about 16 miles north of downtown. Since its inaugural running, the race has been scheduled for the first Sunday of May, the day after the Kentucky Derby, establishing itself as a marquee event on Miami's annual calendar.

Formula One, the highest class of worldwide open-wheel racing sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile, has been considered the pinnacle of motorsport since its inception in 1950. The sport's prestige and global viewership make host cities and their hospitality partners eager to capture spending from international visitors and local enthusiasts during race weekends. According to Digest Miami, the Amazónico and Selva initiative demonstrates how venues are packaging the extended weekend around the actual Grand Prix competition.

The day-to-night structure of the offering—beginning with brunch and extending through evening entertainment—aligns with how major sporting events can anchor multiple revenue opportunities across different dayparts. The rooftop component positions the venues to leverage Miami's year-round outdoor climate and Brickell's central location, while the viewing party ensures that guests without trackside access can experience the race in a curated, social setting.

For Miami's hospitality industry, such coordinated offerings represent a competitive advantage during peak event periods. The success of ventures like this one may influence how other venues structure their programming around future Formula One weekends and similar high-profile sporting occasions.
