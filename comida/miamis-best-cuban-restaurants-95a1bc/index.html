Miami’s Best Cuban Restaurants

What's happening

Eater Miami is highlighting the best Cuban restaurants across the city, recognizing how deeply Cuban cuisine has woven itself into Miami's cultural and culinary identity. The article celebrates how Cuban food has become the backbone of daily life in the region, shaped by decades of immigration waves.

Who's involved

Eater Miami, a food and dining publication, is the source. The story centers on Miami's Cuban community, whose waves of immigration—particularly from the 1960s onward following the Cuban Revolution—established the restaurants and food traditions now defining the city's gastronomic landscape.

Why it matters

Cuban cuisine represents far more than restaurant meals in Miami; it is a living record of cultural resilience and identity shaped by immigration history. Understanding and celebrating these restaurants keeps alive the stories and traditions of Cuban exiles and immigrants while maintaining a vital part of Miami's contemporary culture and economy.

The story

Miami's relationship with Cuban food runs deeper than most cities' connections to any single cuisine. It is woven into the fabric of daily life, a reflection of the waves of Cuban immigrants who have made the city their home since the mid-20th century. According to Eater Miami, Cuban food isn't just popular in Miami—it's part of the city's DNA, a marker of identity as fundamental as the neighborhoods where these communities settled.

The roots of this culinary dominance trace back to two distinct periods of Cuban immigration to the United States. The first wave, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, saw wealthy Cuban Americans establish cigar factories in Tampa, Florida, and participate in movements against Spanish colonial rule. The second, far larger wave came after the Cuban Revolution, when Fidel Castro's rise to power prompted massive migration to Miami. This second surge fundamentally transformed the city's demographics and culture, establishing Cuban immigrants as a dominant force in shaping local society.

Little Havana, the neighborhood that became the symbolic heart of Miami's Cuban community, emerged as the geographic and cultural anchor of this transformation. Named after Havana, Cuba's capital, the neighborhood became home to thousands of Cuban exiles and immigrants who recreated aspects of their former lives while building new ones. Around these neighborhoods, Cuban restaurants evolved from family establishments into institutions, places where food served as both sustenance and continuity with the past.

The restaurants Eater Miami spotlights represent generations of culinary tradition brought across the Florida Strait. Beyond their role as gathering places, these establishments have become economic anchors and cultural ambassadors, drawing residents and visitors alike. The daily rhythm of Miami now pulses to the rhythms established by these Cuban food traditions, from the morning café con leche to the evening meal.

For the Cuban community itself, these restaurants carry weight beyond economics or tourism. They are repositories of memory, places where recipes passed down through families continue to nourish new generations and preserve connections to a homeland many have not seen in decades. The recognition of Miami's best Cuban restaurants is, in essence, recognition of the resilience and creativity of those who built them.
