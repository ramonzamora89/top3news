2028 Bentley Torcal

What's happening

Bentley is preparing to launch the Torcal, its first fully electric vehicle, scheduled to debut in September 2026. The E-segment crossover SUV marks a major shift for the British luxury automaker as it transitions into battery electric powertrains.

Who's involved

Bentley Motors Limited, the British luxury car manufacturer founded in 1919 and now a subsidiary of the Volkswagen Group under Audi's premium brand division since 2022. The company has a storied history of Le Mans racing victories and is headquartered in Crewe, England.

Why it matters

The Torcal represents Bentley's first step into the electric vehicle market during a critical period of industry electrification. For a brand built on performance heritage and exclusivity, the shift to BEV technology signals how even heritage luxury manufacturers must adapt to changing consumer preferences and regulatory pressures around emissions. The 2026 debut will position Bentley competitively within the premium EV crossover segment.

The story

Bentley Motors is moving forward with development of the Torcal, an upcoming battery electric crossover SUV that will mark the company's entry into the fully electric vehicle market. According to Car and Driver, details about the vehicle are beginning to emerge as the automaker prepares for a September 2026 debut.

Founded by W. O. Bentley in 1919, the British luxury marque has spent over a century building its reputation through high-performance vehicles and racing success, including wins at the 24 Hours of Le Mans across six decades. As a subsidiary of the Volkswagen Group since 1998—and consolidated under Audi's premium brand portfolio since 2022—Bentley has access to the VW Group's extensive EV platform technology and battery expertise.

The Torcal will compete in the E-segment, a category dominated by larger crossovers and SUVs aimed at affluent buyers seeking space and presence alongside luxury appointments. By entering this segment with a battery electric vehicle, Bentley is positioning itself alongside other premium brands making similar transitions. The move reflects the broader automotive industry's shift toward electrification, though for Bentley it carries particular significance given the brand's deep roots in combustion-engine performance.

The timing of the 2026 debut gives Bentley approximately two years to finalize engineering, conduct testing, and prepare manufacturing at its Crewe headquarters. As the company's first BEV, the Torcal will establish how Bentley interprets electric mobility through the lens of luxury—a challenge other heritage automakers have tackled with varying approaches to power delivery, range, and charging infrastructure.

With regulatory pressures tightening emissions standards across Europe and major markets worldwide, the Torcal's launch is both a strategic necessity and an opportunity for Bentley to redefine its brand identity for the electric age. The crossover segment's popularity among luxury buyers suggests strong market potential, though success will depend on execution, pricing, and the distinctive character Bentley brings to the category.
