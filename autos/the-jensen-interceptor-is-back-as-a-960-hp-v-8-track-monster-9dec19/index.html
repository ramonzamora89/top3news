The Jensen Interceptor Is Back as a 960-HP V-8 Track Monster

What's happening

Jensen Motors has revived its iconic Interceptor nameplate with a new high-performance variant called the Interceptor GTX, equipped with a 960-horsepower V-8 engine. The car combines classic styling cues from the original model with modern motorsports-inspired aerodynamics and track-focused engineering.

Who's involved

Jensen Motors was a legendary British sports-car manufacturer founded in 1934 by brothers Alan and Richard Jensen in West Bromwich, England, though the company ceased trading in 1976. The brand has since been revived, with this new Interceptor GTX representing a continuation of that legacy.

Why it matters

The return of the Interceptor GTX signals a renewed interest in reviving classic automotive brands with modern performance engineering, appealing to enthusiasts who value heritage alongside contemporary power and capability. This launch demonstrates how defunct manufacturers can re-enter the market by leveraging nostalgic design language paired with cutting-edge drivetrain technology.

The story

Jensen Motors has announced the revival of one of its most storied nameplates with the new Interceptor GTX, a track-focused supercar that marries retro styling with 960 horsepower of V-8 performance. The vehicle marks a significant moment for the British marque, which originally produced the Interceptor as a flagship model during its mid-century heyday before ceasing operations in 1976.

The new GTX honors its predecessor's proportions and visual identity while incorporating modern design elements inspired by motorsports. The car features aerodynamic enhancements engineered for track performance, a dramatic departure from the original model's grand-touring ethos. According to Car and Driver, the vehicle is positioned squarely in the high-performance segment, with its considerable power output and track-oriented engineering suggesting serious performance credentials.

Jensen Motors Limited was established in 1934 when brothers Alan and Richard Jensen rebranded the commercial and sports-car body-making business of W J Smith & Sons Limited. Throughout much of the twentieth century, Jensen built a reputation for producing distinctive British sports cars, though the original company dissolved in 1976 after decades of production. The brand subsequently returned to trading in 1998, indicating the family's commitment to reviving the Jensen namethe, though the original corporate entity was dissolved in 2011.

The Interceptor GTX's resurgence reflects a broader appetite among collectors and driving enthusiasts for cars that blend automotive heritage with contemporary performance standards. By reconnecting modern engineering to a name recognized for classic British craftsmanship, Jensen is positioning itself to compete in the exclusive supercar market where exclusivity and provenance carry significant weight.

The new model's 960-horsepower V-8 engine places it firmly in conversation with established performance brands, while its design language provides immediate visual differentiation rooted in Jensen's archives. Whether the reborn Interceptor succeeds in recapturing the brand's golden era while establishing itself as a serious contemporary performance offering remains to be seen as the vehicle moves toward delivery.
