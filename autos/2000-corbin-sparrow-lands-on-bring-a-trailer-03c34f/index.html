2000 Corbin Sparrow Lands On Bring a Trailer

What's happening

A 2000 Corbin Sparrow, an early electric vehicle from the era when EVs were still novelties, has appeared for sale on Bring a Trailer, the online marketplace for enthusiast vehicles. The listing offers collectors a rare glimpse into automotive history from two decades ago.

Who's involved

Corbin Motors, the manufacturer behind the Sparrow, was an early pioneer in personal electric vehicles during the late 1990s and early 2000s. The company later transitioned to Myers Motors, which continued producing three-wheeled electric commuter vehicles into the 2010s.

Why it matters

The sale highlights how far electric vehicle technology and consumer acceptance have evolved since 2000, when EVs were considered quirky alternatives rather than mainstream options. This particular vehicle serves as a tangible artifact of the EV movement's early, experimental phase, before Tesla and other manufacturers brought the category into the mainstream consciousness.

The story

A 2000 Corbin Sparrow has surfaced on Bring a Trailer, one of the internet's premier marketplaces for unusual and collectible vehicles. According to Car and Driver, back in 2000, EVs were pretty weird—and this particular machine is a perfect embodiment of that era's boldly unconventional approach to electric propulsion.

The Sparrow represents a specific moment in automotive history when manufacturers were exploring radically different form factors for battery-powered vehicles. Corbin Motors designed the Sparrow as a practical urban commuter, though its unconventional three-wheeled design and minimal footprint made it a curiosity rather than a mainstream choice. The vehicle emerged from the same lineage that would later produce the Myers Motors NmG, a single-passenger, three-wheeled battery electric vehicle that the company manufactured from 1999 into the early 2010s specifically for commuting and city driving. After 2005, production shifted to Myers Motors, which continued refining the concept as a personal electric vehicle (PEV) category was still being defined.

What makes the Sparrow's appearance on Bring a Trailer significant is the broader context it provides for understanding EV development. In 2000, the technology was vastly more limited than today—batteries offered shorter ranges, charging infrastructure was virtually nonexistent, and the vehicles themselves carried the stigma of being science-fair experiments rather than serious transportation. Yet manufacturers like Corbin pushed forward with earnest attempts to solve real urban mobility problems, even if their solutions looked bizarre by modern standards.

The sale of this particular 2000 model offers collectors and enthusiasts a tangible piece of that experimental era. Bring a Trailer's platform has become known for surfacing forgotten automotive oddities and giving them a second life with preservationists who recognize their historical value. A Corbin Sparrow from the turn of the millennium falls squarely into that category—it's a vehicle that was ahead of its time conceptually, even if the execution and technology felt alien to most drivers in 2000.

Today's EV market has rendered the Sparrow a historical curio. Modern electric vehicles offer dramatically longer ranges, faster charging, and performance that would have seemed miraculous to Corbin engineers. Yet the Sparrow's existence underscores an important truth: the push toward electrified transportation didn't begin with Elon Musk or recent policy incentives. Visionary manufacturers were already building electric vehicles two decades ago, experimenting with geometry and functionality in ways that feel both earnest and endearingly awkward in retrospect.
