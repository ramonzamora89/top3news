View Photos of the 2001 Roush Stage 3 Mustang

What's happening

Car and Driver is showcasing a visual gallery of the 2001 Roush Stage 3 Mustang, offering detailed photography of both the car's interior and exterior from multiple angles. The feature provides an in-depth look at this high-performance variant of Ford's iconic muscle car.

Who's involved

Roush Performance, the American automotive company founded by Jack Roush in 1995, engineered and developed the Stage 3 package for the 2001 Ford Mustang. The Ford Mustang itself has been in continuous production since 1964 and serves as the namesake of the entire "pony car" segment.

Why it matters

This visual documentation preserves the design and engineering details of a significant performance variant from the early 2000s, offering enthusiasts and historians a comprehensive record of Roush's modifications to one of America's most recognizable automobiles. For collectors and fans of both the Mustang and Roush Performance, such galleries serve as valuable references for understanding the evolution of street performance engineering during that era.

The story

Car and Driver presents an extensive photographic tour of the 2001 Roush Stage 3 Mustang, capturing the performance-enhanced muscle car from every conceivable angle. The gallery showcases both the vehicle's aggressive exterior styling and its carefully appointed interior, offering enthusiasts a detailed visual record of this significant performance variant.

Roush Performance, founded in 1995 by automotive mogul Jack Roush, has built its reputation on engineering and manufacturing high-performance components for both street and competitive racing applications. The Stage 3 package represented the company's commitment to extracting maximum performance from the Ford Mustang platform, a car that has maintained continuous production since 1964 and effectively created the "pony car" segment that defines American automotive culture.

The 2001 model year placed the Roush Stage 3 variant during a transitional period for the Mustang, bridging the fourth generation that had debuted in 1994 and the eventual introduction of the fifth generation in 2005. During this era, Roush's aftermarket enhancements allowed performance-focused buyers to elevate their Mustangs beyond factory specifications, whether for street driving or track use.

According to Car and Driver, the photographic documentation includes comprehensive views of the vehicle's exterior modifications, interior trim, and performance details. Such galleries serve an important archival function, preserving the specific design language and engineering choices of a particular moment in automotive performance history. For collectors, prospective buyers, and automotive historians, these visual records provide invaluable reference materials.

The emphasis on multi-angle photography reflects the growing importance of visual documentation in automotive media. By presenting the 2001 Roush Stage 3 Mustang from every perspective, the feature allows viewers to appreciate both the aesthetics of Roush's modifications and the practical engineering that distinguishes high-performance variants from standard production models.
