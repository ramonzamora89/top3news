Who Teaches a Robotaxi’s Remote Helpers? SAE Has a Driver’s Ed Plan

What's happening

SAE International is developing a formal training and certification program for remote operators who control Level 4 and Level 5 autonomous vehicles. The initiative addresses a growing need to standardize skills and qualifications for workers managing robotaxis and other driverless fleets from remote locations.

Who's involved

SAE International, a global professional association and standards organization based in Warrendale, Pennsylvania, is spearheading the effort. The organization, which expanded beyond its original automotive engineering focus to include aerospace and autonomous vehicle standards, is positioning itself as the authority for operator certification.

Why it matters

As autonomous vehicle fleets scale up, standardizing operator training becomes critical for safety, liability, and workforce development. Remote operation of robotaxis represents an entirely new job category, and establishing clear educational pathways could shape how the autonomous vehicle industry manages human oversight and emergency response.

The story

SAE International is creating a structured training and certification program for remote operators of autonomous vehicles, marking a significant step toward professionalizing a workforce that barely existed a few years ago. The initiative, according to Car and Driver, reflects the urgent need to establish consistent standards as Level 4 and Level 5 autonomous vehicle deployments expand beyond pilot programs into commercial operations at scale.

Remote operators of robotaxis and autonomous fleets perform a specialized function that differs fundamentally from traditional driving. Rather than controlling the vehicle moment-to-moment, these workers monitor operations from a distance and intervene when needed—responding to technical failures, assisting with edge cases the vehicle's artificial intelligence cannot resolve, or taking control during unexpected situations. The job demands a hybrid skill set that blends traditional automotive knowledge with technology proficiency and situational judgment.

SAE International, which evolved from the Society of Automotive Engineers to encompass a broader scope including aerospace and commercial autonomous vehicles, has the technical credibility and industry reach to establish such standards. The organization's involvement signals that remote operator training is being treated as a professional discipline rather than an ad hoc onboarding process individual companies devise internally. Standardization across the industry could improve safety consistency and worker mobility, allowing certified operators to transition between employers more easily.

The timing of this initiative reflects market realities. Companies deploying robotaxis at scale—from Waymo to emerging autonomous vehicle startups—are already hiring remote operators, but without agreed-upon training frameworks, each organization trains workers differently. A formal curriculum would establish baseline competencies, define escalation procedures, and create measurable performance metrics across the industry.

Developing curriculum for remote vehicle operation presents unique pedagogical challenges. Traditional driver's education focuses on vehicle control and road rules; remote operator training must emphasize system monitoring, decision-making under ambiguity, and crisis management in real time. SAE's program will likely combine classroom instruction on autonomous vehicle architecture, simulation-based practice scenarios, and supervised operational experience.

As autonomous vehicles transition from experimental technology to commercial infrastructure, the workers enabling that transition deserve formal recognition and pathways for advancement. SAE's driver's education plan for remote operators acknowledges that professionalism and standardization will be essential to public trust and operational reliability.
