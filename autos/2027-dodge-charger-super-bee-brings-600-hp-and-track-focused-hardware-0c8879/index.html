2027 Dodge Charger Super Bee Brings 600 HP and Track-Focused Hardware

What's happening

Dodge has unveiled the 2027 Charger Super Bee, a track-focused muscle car powered by a 600-horsepower six-cylinder engine. The new model marks a revival of the Super Bee nameplate and introduces performance-oriented hardware designed to deliver faster lap times.

Who's involved

Dodge, the American automaker owned by Stellantis, is behind the 2027 Super Bee revival. The company has been marketing the Charger since 1966 across eight generations, and is now resurrecting the Super Bee badge, which was last produced as a muscle car in 1971.

Why it matters

The 2027 Super Bee signals Dodge's commitment to performance vehicles in an era of electrification, offering enthusiasts a six-cylinder alternative with significant horsepower. The track-focused engineering represents an effort to compete in the high-performance segment while maintaining the muscle-car heritage that defined the original Super Bee era.

The story

Dodge has reignited one of muscle-car history's most iconic nameplates with the introduction of the 2027 Charger Super Bee, a track-ready vehicle that delivers 600 horsepower from a six-cylinder powertrain. According to Car and Driver, this new iteration represents the most powerful six-cylinder Charger ever built, combining classic branding with contemporary performance engineering.

The Super Bee badge carries significant weight in automotive history. Originally marketed by Dodge as a mid-sized muscle car from 1968 through 1971, the Super Bee became synonymous with American performance and aggressive styling. The nameplate also enjoyed a extended run in Mexico, where it was based on a compact Chrysler platform and remained in production from 1970 until 1980. By reviving the Super Bee name for the modern Charger lineup, Dodge is tapping into decades of brand recognition and nostalgia among enthusiasts.

The 2027 model introduces a suite of track-focused hardware engineered to maximize performance on the circuit. While specific technical details remain limited, the emphasis on lap-time improvements suggests the Super Bee incorporates enhanced suspension geometry, braking systems, and aerodynamic refinements beyond the standard Charger platform. This approach positions the Super Bee as a driver-oriented vehicle rather than a purely casual cruiser.

The six-cylinder engine choice reflects Dodge's strategic direction in an automotive landscape increasingly concerned with efficiency and emissions. Rather than relying on traditional large-displacement V8 powerplants, the 600-horsepower six-cylinder demonstrates that manufacturers can achieve substantial performance through advanced engineering. This represents a pragmatic balance between maintaining the muscle-car identity that customers expect and meeting modern regulatory requirements.

The Charger itself has maintained a prominent place in Dodge's lineup since its 1966 debut, spanning eight generations of evolution. The new Super Bee variant adds a performance-oriented tier to that legacy, appealing to buyers who prioritize track capability and distinctive branding. As the automotive industry continues its transition toward electrification, offerings like the Super Bee may represent a final chapter for traditional high-performance internal-combustion vehicles from major manufacturers.
