2027 Lamborghini Revuelto SV Gains More Power and Downforce

What's happening

Lamborghini is introducing a track-focused Super Veloce variant of its Revuelto V12 hybrid supercar for 2027. The new SV model adds a fixed rear wing and manually adjustable dampers to boost performance and handling on the circuit.

Who's involved

Lamborghini, the Italian luxury sports car manufacturer based in Sant'Agata Bolognese and owned by the Volkswagen Group through its Audi subsidiary, is developing the Revuelto SV. The model builds on the standard Revuelto, the brand's flagship V12 hybrid supercar.

Why it matters

The SV designation signals Lamborghini's commitment to creating dedicated high-performance variants that appeal to track enthusiasts and collectors seeking extreme versions of its hypercars. This move reflects the company's strategy of leveraging its V12 hybrid powertrain across multiple market segments, from road-focused to circuit-ready applications.

The story

Lamborghini is reviving its Super Veloce nameplate with a track-tuned version of the Revuelto supercar arriving in 2027. According to Car and Driver, the Revuelto SV adds a fixed rear wing and manually adjustable dampers to the company's flagship V12 hybrid, transforming the grand-touring machine into a circuit-ready weapon with greater power output and aerodynamic downforce.

The Super Veloce badge carries significant historical weight in Lamborghini's lineage, historically applied to the most aggressive, performance-oriented versions of the brand's most celebrated models. By applying it to the Revuelto, the Italian manufacturer signals its intention to push the hybrid supercar beyond its original grand-touring purpose into pure track territory. The fixed rear wing replaces the variable wing system found on the standard Revuelto, trading adjustability for raw aerodynamic efficiency at high speeds.

The manually adjustable damper system represents another shift toward driver engagement and track optimization. Unlike the adaptive systems in the base model, fixed damper settings allow drivers to dial in their preferred suspension tune for specific circuit conditions. Lamborghini has not yet released specific power figures for the SV variant, but the announcement confirms that the new model will produce more horsepower than the standard Revuelto.

Automobili Lamborghini S.p.A., as the company is formally known, has built its reputation on combining dramatic design with cutting-edge engineering. The Revuelto itself marked a watershed moment for the brand, introducing a V12 engine—a twelve-cylinder configuration with two banks of six cylinders arranged in a V around a common crankshaft—alongside hybrid technology for the first time in the company's history.

The SV variant arrives as automakers increasingly segment their high-performance lineups between comfort-focused and track-focused versions. By offering a dedicated Super Veloce model, Lamborghini provides collectors and drivers with a choice tailored to their priorities: the standard Revuelto for everyday supercar duties and long-distance travel, and the SV for those seeking maximum circuit performance and bragging rights among peers.
