Next-Gen Mitsubishi Montero Will Soon Make Its Global Debut

What's happening

Mitsubishi is preparing to unveil the next-generation Montero (known as the Pajero in some markets) on September 2. The new model will roll out globally, though North American customers won't see it arrive until closer to 2030.

Who's involved

Mitsubishi Motors Corporation, a Japanese multinational automaker headquartered in Tokyo, is leading the effort. Since 2016, Mitsubishi has been one-third owned by Nissan as part of the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance, giving the company additional backing for major product launches.

Why it matters

The Montero's return marks a significant milestone for Mitsubishi after the nameplate was discontinued in 2021. The delayed North American launch underscores the automaker's strategic focus on other markets first, while the global debut signals renewed confidence in the full-size SUV segment even as the industry shifts toward electrification.

The story

Mitsubishi Motors is preparing to make a major announcement this month with the global debut of the next-generation Montero on September 2. The full-size SUV, which carries the Pajero nameplate in certain markets, represents a significant return for the Japanese automaker after the model was discontinued in 2021. According to Car and Driver, the vehicle will reach North American showrooms considerably later, with availability not expected until closer to 2030.

The Montero's lineage stretches back four decades. Originally introduced in 1981, the model has undergone multiple generational updates and established itself as a cornerstone of Mitsubishi's global lineup. Its discontinuation in 2021 marked the end of an era, but the company has now committed to reviving the nameplate with a fresh design and updated technology.

Mitsubishi's staged rollout strategy reflects broader patterns in how automakers are managing new model introductions across different regions. By debuting the vehicle globally first and then staggering North American availability by several years, the company gains time to refine production, assess market demand, and potentially develop market-specific variants. This approach also allows Mitsubishi to prioritize other markets where demand for full-size SUVs remains particularly strong.

The timing of the announcement comes as Mitsubishi continues to operate as a major player in the global automotive landscape. As of 2012, the company ranked as the 16th-largest automaker worldwide by production. More recently, Mitsubishi has been integrated into the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance, with Nissan holding a 34 percent stake since October 2016. This partnership provides capital and engineering resources that support ambitious product launches like the Montero revival.

The delayed North American arrival reflects the reality that full-size SUVs occupy a different market position in different regions. While the segment remains important in North America, other markets prioritize these vehicles differently. By waiting until closer to 2030, Mitsubishi can align the launch with evolving consumer preferences, potentially incorporating electrification or hybrid technology that may become more standard by that time.
