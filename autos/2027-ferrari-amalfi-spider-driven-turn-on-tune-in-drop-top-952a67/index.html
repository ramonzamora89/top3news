2027 Ferrari Amalfi Spider Driven: Turn On, Tune In, Drop Top

What's happening

Ferrari has unveiled the 2027 Amalfi Spider, its newest entry-level GT convertible, positioning it as a vehicle designed for drivers who prioritize living in the present moment. Car and Driver has driven the new model and filed their impressions.

Who's involved

Ferrari S.p.A., the Italian luxury sports car manufacturer based in Maranello, is behind the Amalfi Spider. Founded by Enzo Ferrari in 1939 and spun off as an independent public company in 2016, Ferrari continues to expand its road car lineup with new models targeting different buyer segments.

Why it matters

The Amalfi Spider represents Ferrari's strategy to broaden its market reach with an accessible convertible GT offering, potentially attracting buyers seeking the brand's prestige in a more lifestyle-focused package. As an entry-level model, it signals how even luxury automakers are balancing performance credentials with approachability in an evolving market.

The story

Ferrari's latest addition to its convertible lineup, the 2027 Amalfi Spider, arrives as the brand's newest entry-level grand tourer with a design philosophy centered on present-moment driving. According to Car and Driver, the vehicle prioritizes an engaging, immersive experience that encourages owners to focus on the act of driving rather than accumulating features or specifications.

The Amalfi Spider continues Ferrari's long tradition of accessible yet desirable vehicles. Since Ferrari began producing its current line of road cars in 1947, the manufacturer has refined the art of creating entry-level models that maintain the brand's performance and design DNA while broadening appeal. The convertible format has remained a cornerstone of Ferrari's portfolio, offering buyers the freedom of open-air driving combined with grand touring capability.

As an entry-level GT, the Amalfi Spider occupies a strategic position in Ferrari's model range. The car emphasizes the sensory elements of driving—engine response, handling feel, and the connection between driver and machine—rather than technological excess. This design direction reflects a broader philosophy that values mechanical engagement and real-time driver feedback, elements central to Ferrari's identity since Enzo Ferrari's founding vision in 1939.

The convertible format adds a distinctive dimension to the entry-level segment, allowing drivers to experience the Italian landscape and road conditions directly. This openness to the environment aligns with the car's philosophical approach: removing barriers between driver and experience, encouraging focus on driving dynamics rather than distraction.

For Ferrari, now operating as a fully independent company since its 2016 spinoff from Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, the Amalfi Spider represents a calculated effort to maintain accessibility within the luxury sports car category. The model aims to attract buyers seeking Ferrari's heritage and performance credentials without requiring the investment level of higher-tier offerings. The emphasis on present-moment engagement rather than technological ostentation distinguishes this entry into an increasingly crowded convertible GT market.
