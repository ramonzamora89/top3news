We Test Chevy’s New Small-Block V-8 in the 2027 Corvette Grand Sport

What's happening

Car and Driver has tested Chevrolet's redesigned 6.7-liter LS6 small-block V-8 engine in the upcoming 2027 Corvette Grand Sport. The new powerplant delivers performance that slots between the base LT2 Stingray and the higher-end Z06 model in terms of 0-60 acceleration.

Who's involved

Chevrolet, the American sports car manufacturer under General Motors, continues its legacy with the Corvette line, which has been a hallmark of American two-seater performance since 1953. Car and Driver, a major automotive publication, conducted the testing and evaluation.

Why it matters

The new engine represents Chevrolet's strategy to expand the Corvette lineup with additional performance tiers, giving buyers more options within the C8 generation. The positioning of the Grand Sport as a middle-ground performer could broaden appeal to customers seeking more power than the base model without committing to the premium Z06 variant.

The story

Chevrolet has refreshed its small-block engine lineup for the 2027 model year, introducing a new 6.7-liter LS6 to power the upcoming Corvette Grand Sport. According to Car and Driver, the engine delivers performance metrics that place it squarely between two existing variants: quicker than the LT2-equipped Stingray but trailing the more potent Z06 model in acceleration to 60 mph.

The Chevrolet small-block engine has long been a cornerstone of the automaker's performance strategy, evolving through several generations of non-LS designs before transitioning to the modern LS-based architecture. The new LS6 represents the latest iteration in this lineage, combining proven engineering principles with refinements aimed at the mid-tier Grand Sport segment.

The Grand Sport nameplate carries historical significance within the Corvette family, traditionally occupying a performance position above the base model but distinct from the most hardcore variants. By pairing the new 6.7-liter LS6 with this model, Chevrolet is clearly targeting buyers who want tangible power increases without stepping up to the full expense and capability of the Z06.

Since its introduction in 1953, the Corvette has maintained its status as America's only domestically mass-produced two-seater sports car, consistently noted for its performance, distinctive styling, and competitive pricing relative to imported rivals. The expansion of the engine and trim lineup for the current generation demonstrates Chevrolet's continued commitment to offering choice within the sports car segment.

The acceleration data from Car and Driver's testing provides concrete evidence of where the Grand Sport fits in the Corvette hierarchy. Sitting between the approachable LT2 and the extreme Z06, the new LS6-powered Grand Sport offers a compelling option for enthusiasts seeking a performance upgrade without the complexity, cost, or raw intensity of the lineup's most aggressive offering.
