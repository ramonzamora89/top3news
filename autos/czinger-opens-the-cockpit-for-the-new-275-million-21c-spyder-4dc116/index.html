Czinger Opens the Cockpit for the New $2.75 Million 21C Spyder

What's happening

Czinger has unveiled a new roadster variant of its 21C hypercar, priced at $2.75 million. The open-top model retains the radical 3-D-printed construction of the original while delivering hypercar-class performance, achieving zero-to-60-mph acceleration in rocket-like time.

Who's involved

Czinger is a Los Angeles-based hypercar manufacturer known for pushing the boundaries of automotive design and manufacturing through advanced 3-D printing technology. The company specializes in ultra-exclusive, high-performance vehicles that blend cutting-edge materials science with extreme engineering.

Why it matters

The introduction of a roadster variant expands the appeal of 3-D-printed hypercar technology beyond closed-cockpit purists, potentially signaling broader acceptance of additive manufacturing in ultra-luxury performance vehicles. This move demonstrates that radical manufacturing techniques can scale across vehicle configurations while maintaining the extreme performance characteristics that define the hypercar segment.

The story

Czinger has opened the cockpit of its radical 21C hypercar with the introduction of a new Spyder variant, expanding its lineup of 3-D-printed hypercars to include an open-air option. The new roadster carries a price tag of $2.75 million and promises the same rocket-like performance as its closed-cockpit predecessor, achieving zero-to-60-mph acceleration in the hypercar-defining sub-three-second range.

The 21C Spyder represents a significant engineering achievement in translating Czinger's signature construction method—extensive use of 3-D printing for chassis and body components—into a convertible format. The technology, which has become a hallmark of the brand's approach to reducing weight while maintaining structural integrity, allows for dramatic weight savings compared to traditionally manufactured hypercars. According to Car and Driver, this roadster variant maintains the performance envelope that has positioned the 21C as a contender in the ultra-exclusive hypercar market.

The hypercar category itself has become increasingly defined by such technological innovation. Racing enthusiasts may recognize the hypercar designation from the FIA World Endurance Championship, where Le Mans Hypercar represents a pinnacle of sports racing car engineering. While Czinger's 21C operates in the road-car segment rather than competitive racing, it embodies similar principles of extreme performance, lightweight construction, and cutting-edge materials science that characterize the broader hypercar category.

The decision to offer the 21C as a roadster expands Czinger's appeal beyond drivers seeking an enclosed cockpit. Open-air hypercars represent a rare niche within an already ultra-exclusive market segment, potentially attracting collectors and enthusiasts who prioritize the sensory experience of driving alongside raw performance metrics. The Spyder variant maintains the dramatic design language that made the original 21C a striking presence in automotive showrooms and at exclusive events.

With only a handful of hypercars produced globally each year, the introduction of a roadster option underscores the company's confidence in its 3-D-printed construction methodology and performance benchmarks. The $2.75 million price point positions the Spyder competitively within the hypercar market, where pricing typically ranges from $2 million to more than $5 million depending on specifications and exclusivity.

The launch of the 21C Spyder marks another milestone in Czinger's evolution as a manufacturer willing to challenge conventional automotive production methods. As additive manufacturing technology continues to mature, such applications in ultra-high-performance vehicles may demonstrate pathways for the broader industry to adopt similar manufacturing innovations.
