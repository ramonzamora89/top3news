McLaren Teases a New Car with a Manual Shifter Inspired by the M6GT

What's happening

McLaren Automotive is teasing a new car featuring a manual transmission shifter, drawing inspiration from Bruce McLaren's iconic M6GT prototype. The company revealed the tease through social media, blending archival footage of the classic race car with new footage of the upcoming model.

Who's involved

McLaren Automotive, the British luxury sports car manufacturer based in Woking, England, is behind the tease. The company is channeling the legacy of Bruce McLaren, the New Zealand racing driver and automotive designer who founded the marque and famously won the 1966 24 Hours of Le Mans and multiple Canadian-American Challenge Cup titles.

Why it matters

The return of a manual transmission in a modern McLaren signals a potential shift away from the industry-wide adoption of automatic transmissions in high-performance vehicles. This move could appeal to driving enthusiasts who prize mechanical engagement, while also honoring the hands-on driving ethos that defined the original McLaren racing era.

The story

McLaren Automotive is stoking enthusiasm for an upcoming model by showcasing a manual transmission shifter in a new teaser campaign. The British automaker posted social media content combining archival footage from Bruce McLaren's M6GT prototype—a legendary race car from the 1960s—with fresh video of a new vehicle equipped with a manual gearbox, according to Car and Driver. The juxtaposition creates a direct lineage between the company's racing heritage and its modern design philosophy.

Bruce McLaren, the company's founder, was a New Zealand racing driver, engineer, and motorsport executive who competed in Formula One from 1958 to 1970 and achieved significant success in sports car racing. His 1966 victory at the 24 Hours of Le Mans with Chris Amon in a Ford GT40, and his back-to-back Canadian-American Challenge Cup wins in 1967 and 1969, cemented his reputation as both a competitor and innovator. The M6GT prototype, referenced in the teaser, represents that era of driver-centric automotive engineering when manual control was paramount.

Manual transmissions have largely disappeared from the modern supercar market, with manufacturers opting for dual-clutch and automated systems that deliver faster gear changes and improved performance metrics. McLaren's decision to highlight a manual shifter in its teaser suggests the company may be reconsidering this trend, at least for select models. The move reflects growing nostalgia among driving purists for the mechanical feedback and engagement that manual gearboxes provide, even as performance benchmarks favor automation.

McLaren Automotive, now wholly owned by CYVN Holdings LLC—the investment vehicle of Abu Dhabi's government as of March 2025—continues to produce sports cars at its Technology Centre in Woking, England. The company has built its reputation on in-house manufacturing and cutting-edge design, and the teased manual-transmission model appears to blend that forward-thinking approach with a deliberate nod to its founder's driving philosophy.

Full details about the new car remain under wraps, with only the teaser video and manual shifter visible so far. The campaign's emphasis on Bruce McLaren's legacy and the M6GT suggests the automaker wants to position this vehicle as a return to fundamentals—a celebration of the human element in driving at a time when electrification and automation dominate the industry conversation.
