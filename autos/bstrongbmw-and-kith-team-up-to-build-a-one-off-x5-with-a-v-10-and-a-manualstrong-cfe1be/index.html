<b><strong>BMW and Kith Team Up to Build a One-Off X5 with a V-10 and a Manual</strong></b>

What's happening

BMW has partnered with fashion and lifestyle brand Kith to create a unique one-off X5 that combines a V-10 engine with a manual transmission—a rare combination in modern SUVs. This bespoke build represents an unusual meeting of automotive performance and luxury lifestyle branding.

Who's involved

BMW is the German luxury automaker that pioneered the crossover segment with the original X5 in 1999. Kith is a New York-based fashion and lifestyle brand known for high-end collaborations across multiple industries, including automotive partnerships.

Why it matters

The project highlights growing demand among collectors and enthusiasts for distinctive, manually-shifted luxury vehicles at a time when such drivetrains are nearly extinct in production cars. It also demonstrates how legacy automakers are exploring creative partnerships to maintain brand relevance and appeal to niche markets beyond traditional marketing channels.

The story

BMW and the lifestyle brand Kith have joined forces to create a one-of-a-kind X5 that reimagines what a luxury crossover can be, equipping it with a V-10 engine paired to a manual transmission—a powertrain combination that feels increasingly rare in today's automotive landscape. The build celebrates the appeal of driver engagement in a segment long defined by convenience and automatic gearboxes.

The X5, which made its debut in 1999 as the E53 model, represented BMW's first serious entry into the booming SUV market and proved prescient in predicting the segment's eventual dominance in global sales. Early iterations of the X5 were available with either a manual or automatic gearbox, though that flexibility disappeared as the model evolved. The second-generation E70, launched in 2006, featured only an automatic transmission paired with BMW's xDrive all-wheel-drive system, a direction the brand has maintained ever since.

This collaboration with Kith arrives at a moment when manual transmissions have become luxury items themselves. The V-10 engine—likely sourced from BMW's performance division—pairs raw mechanical power with the direct mechanical connection that a three-pedal setup provides. According to Car and Driver, the resulting one-off is as appealing as the X5 segment gets, bridging the gap between everyday practicality and driver-focused performance in ways that factory options rarely attempt.

The partnership reflects a broader shift in how premium brands engage with niche audiences. Rather than rely solely on traditional advertising, BMW and Kith are creating a conversation piece—a vehicle that appeals to both automotive enthusiasts and the lifestyle brand's fashion-forward clientele. The exclusivity of a single build, combined with the mechanical authenticity of a manual gearbox and high-performance engine, taps into a collector's mentality that values rarity and substance over mass-market convenience.

While the one-off nature of this project means it will remain a curiosity rather than a market indicator, it underscores enduring appetite among certain buyers for vehicles that prioritize engagement and mechanical honesty. The X5's evolution from a first-generation model available with manual gears to a segment dominated by slick automatics illustrates how far the industry has drifted from driver-centric design—making this Kith collaboration a potent reminder of what premium driving can still feel like.
