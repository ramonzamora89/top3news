2027 Chevy Express and GMC Savana Vans Finally Get Touchscreens

What's happening

General Motors is finally equipping its 2027 Chevy Express and GMC Savana commercial vans with touchscreen displays, a technology standard in most vehicles for over a decade. The upgrade marks a significant modernization of GM's aging van lineup.

Who's involved

General Motors Company, headquartered in Detroit, Michigan, is the largest automaker in the United States by total sales and owns four major automobile brands: Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, and Cadillac. The Chevrolet Express has been in continuous production since 1996 as a full-size van available in both passenger and cargo variants, including specialized cutaway chassis for commercial applications like ambulances and motorhomes.

Why it matters

The addition of touchscreen technology brings the Express and Savana closer to feature parity with competing commercial and consumer vehicles, potentially improving driver experience and reducing the perception that these work vehicles are technologically outdated. This upgrade may help GM maintain competitiveness in the commercial van market, where buyers increasingly expect modern infotainment systems as standard amenities.

The story

General Motors is modernizing two of its longest-serving commercial van lines by introducing touchscreen displays to the 2027 Chevrolet Express and GMC Savana. The addition represents a long-overdue technology upgrade for vehicles that have continued largely unchanged in their core infotainment offerings while the broader automotive industry embraced digital displays as a baseline feature.

The Chevrolet Express, produced by GM since 1996, has become a cornerstone of the commercial vehicle market, available in passenger and cargo configurations. Beyond the standard van body, the Express lineup includes cutaway van chassis designed for specialized commercial applications—ambulances, buses, motorhomes, and small trucks—making it a versatile platform for fleet operators and specialty vehicle builders. The GMC Savana serves a parallel role within GM's GMC brand. Despite their prevalence on American roads and their importance to GM's commercial customer base, both vans have lagged significantly behind consumer expectations for in-vehicle technology.

Touchscreen interfaces have been standard equipment in most passenger vehicles for well over a decade, making their absence in the Express and Savana increasingly conspicuous. According to Car and Driver, this tech upgrade finally brings the vans into alignment with modern vehicle standards. The delay underscores how commercial vehicle development cycles can diverge from consumer automotive trends, particularly when fleet buyers prioritize durability and cost-effectiveness over amenities.

The 2027 model year update signals GM's recognition that commercial operators and drivers now expect modern conveniences regardless of vehicle category. For fleet managers, touchscreen interfaces can simplify vehicle operation, reduce training requirements for new drivers, and improve perceived brand value. The upgrade may also help attract younger drivers to commercial vehicle roles, where technology expectations have shifted considerably in recent years.

General Motors remains the largest automaker in the United States by total sales, though it held the global sales title for 77 years before Toyota surpassed it in 2008. The company's continued investment in modernizing its commercial vehicle offerings reflects an effort to maintain leadership in segments where it has historically dominated, even as competition intensifies and buyer expectations evolve.
