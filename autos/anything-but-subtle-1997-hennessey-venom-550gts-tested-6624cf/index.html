Anything but Subtle: 1997 Hennessey Venom 550GTS Tested

What's happening

Hennessey Performance Engineering has tested its 1997 Venom 550GTS, a modified Dodge Viper that extracts an additional 100 horsepower from the already potent 8.0-liter V10 engine. The tuning company is showcasing what this upgrade delivers on the road.

Who's involved

Hennessey Performance Engineering is an American automotive tuning company and sports car manufacturer known for high-performance modifications. The Dodge Viper, which forms the foundation for this build, is an American two-seat sports car that began production in 1991 at New Mack Assembly Plant and later moved to Conner Avenue Assembly Plant in October 1995.

Why it matters

The Venom 550GTS represents the kind of extreme performance engineering that pushes production-car capability to its limits, offering buyers a path to supercar-level power without starting from scratch. For enthusiasts of American muscle and sports cars, the ability to add 100 horses to an already aggressive V10 demonstrates the depth of tuning potential in 1990s platform engineering.

The story

Hennessey Performance Engineering has tested its 1997 Venom 550GTS, a heavily modified Dodge Viper that demonstrates the company's ability to extract serious performance gains from already formidable machinery. According to Car and Driver, the upgrade adds another 100 horsepower to the Viper's 8.0-liter V10 engine, pushing the sports car into rarefied performance territory.

The Dodge Viper itself arrived as a bold statement when production began in 1991 at New Mack Assembly Plant. Drawing inspiration from the classic AC Cobra, the two-seat roadster was conceived as a raw, unfiltered American sports car that prioritized engine displacement and straightforward mechanical feedback over technological sophistication. The model's production shifted to Conner Avenue Assembly Plant in October 1995, by which time the Viper had already established itself as a car that eschewed subtlety in favor of pure, unbridled power.

Hennessey's approach to the 1997 model builds on that philosophy. Rather than rework the Viper's fundamental character, the tuning company has focused on maximizing what the 8.0-liter naturally aspirated V10 can deliver. The addition of 100 horsepower is substantial—a figure that would constitute an entire modern compact car's output in isolation—and speaks to both the engine's inherent potential and Hennessey's tuning expertise.

The Venom 550GTS designation signals the performance tier Hennessey has achieved, positioning the car as a step above stock. For buyers seeking to differentiate their Vipers or extract maximum performance from the platform, such modifications represented one of the few avenues available during the 1990s to rival or exceed exotic-car performance without importing European machinery.

The test results validate what the modifications promise: a car that combines the Viper's visceral character with additional grunt that makes it even more aggressive at speed. This approach—taking an already extreme production car and pushing it further—reflected a particular moment in American automotive culture when power gains measured in triple digits were achievable through mechanical means rather than electronic management.
