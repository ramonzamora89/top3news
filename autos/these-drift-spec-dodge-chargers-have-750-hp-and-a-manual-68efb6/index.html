These Drift-Spec Dodge Chargers Have 750 HP and a Manual

What's happening

Dodge has unveiled two custom-built Chargers stripped down and rebuilt as dedicated drift cars, each packing 750 horsepower and equipped with a manual transmission. These one-off vehicles transform the standard Scat Pack performance lineup into machines purpose-built for controlled slides and extreme cornering.

Who's involved

Dodge, the American automotive brand and division of Stellantis based in Auburn Hills, Michigan, created these vehicles. Dodge has a long history of producing performance cars and has been marketing the Charger model across eight generations since 1966.

Why it matters

The drift-spec Chargers demonstrate Dodge's continued commitment to performance customization and manual transmission options at a time when automatics dominate the market. The move signals the brand's engagement with drift culture and enthusiasts seeking hardcore, track-focused versions of production vehicles.

The story

Dodge has taken two of its Scat Pack Chargers and transformed them into purpose-built drift machines, each delivering 750 horsepower and equipped with a manual transmission. According to Car and Driver, these are one-off builds, meaning they represent custom, unique creations rather than a production line expansion.

The Charger has been a cornerstone of Dodge's performance portfolio since its introduction in 1966, and the brand has long positioned itself as an American manufacturer of high-output vehicles. The Scat Pack lineup represents the modern iteration of that performance heritage, and converting these models into dedicated drift platforms extends that legacy into specialized motorsport territory.

Drift racing demands specific mechanical modifications beyond what factory performance models offer. A manual transmission is essential for the heel-and-toe downshifting and throttle control that drift technique requires, making the inclusion of a stick shift particularly notable on a modern muscle car. The 750-horsepower figure indicates substantial engine tuning and optimization, likely including upgrades to forced induction, fuel delivery, and internal engine components.

These one-off builds suggest Dodge's interest in demonstrating what its performance platform can achieve when pushed to extremes. Rather than releasing a production drift variant, the company has created showcase vehicles that can be used for promotional purposes, motorsport events, or customer experiences. This approach allows Dodge to explore niche performance segments without the investment required for a full production run.

The drift-spec Chargers also reflect broader enthusiasm within the automotive community for manual transmissions and analog driving experiences. As many manufacturers phase out stick shifts in favor of automatics, builds like these appeal to purists and enthusiasts who value driver engagement and the tactile feedback of a clutch pedal.
