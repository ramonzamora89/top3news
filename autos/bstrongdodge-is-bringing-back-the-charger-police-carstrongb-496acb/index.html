<b><strong>Dodge Is Bringing Back the Charger Police Car</strong></b>

What's happening

Dodge is preparing to reintroduce the Charger as a police vehicle, potentially returning the iconic muscle car to patrol duties starting next year. The move marks a revival of the "Officer Mopar" era when Dodge vehicles served in law enforcement roles.

Who's involved

Dodge is an American automotive brand and division of Stellantis, based in Auburn Hills, Michigan, with a long history of producing performance vehicles. The Dodge Charger is a model that has been marketed across eight generations since 1966 and was historically used by police departments.

Why it matters

The return of the Charger police car could reshape law enforcement fleet purchasing decisions and signal Dodge's renewed commitment to the police vehicle market. For Dodge, it represents an opportunity to leverage the Charger's performance heritage and brand recognition to compete in a specialized but meaningful segment of automotive sales.

The story

Dodge appears poised to bring one of American policing's most storied vehicles back to the streets. According to Car and Driver, the automaker is planning to reintroduce the Dodge Charger as a police car option beginning next year, reviving what was once a fixture in patrol fleets across the country under the "Officer Mopar" banner.

The Dodge Charger has been a model of the brand since 1966, spanning eight generations and becoming synonymous with high-performance driving. The vehicle's muscular design and powerful engines made it a natural fit for law enforcement, where patrol cars must balance speed and durability. The decision to bring it back suggests that police departments may be looking to move away from the sedan-based patrol vehicles that have dominated the market in recent years.

Dodge, a division of Stellantis headquartered in Auburn Hills, Michigan, has historically positioned itself as a brand centered on performance vehicles. The revival of the Charger police car fits squarely within that heritage, offering departments an alternative to conventional patrol cars. By bringing the model back for law enforcement use, Dodge is betting that police agencies will value the distinctive look and driving dynamics of the Charger alongside its practical capabilities.

The timing of the announcement reflects broader shifts in how police departments procure vehicles. As concerns about vehicle performance and officer safety continue to influence purchasing decisions, the reintroduction of a high-performance option like the Charger could appeal to departments seeking to differentiate their fleets. Whether this marks a sustained return to muscle car-based police vehicles or a limited revival remains to be seen.
