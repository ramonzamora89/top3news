By 2029, Ford's Entry-Level Model Will Reportedly Be a $25K SUV

What's happening

Ford is planning to launch a new entry-level SUV by 2029 with a starting price of $25,000. The company displayed early mockups of the vehicle at a dealer event, featuring both hybrid and nonhybrid powertrain options.

Who's involved

Ford Motor Company, the American multinational automobile manufacturer headquartered in Dearborn, Michigan, is spearheading this initiative. Founded by Henry Ford in 1903 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker F, the company sells vehicles under the Ford brand as well as luxury cars under its Lincoln brand.

Why it matters

The $25,000 entry-level SUV could reshape Ford's position in the affordable vehicle segment, directly competing with mass-market alternatives as consumer demand for SUVs continues to dominate the market. The inclusion of hybrid options signals Ford's commitment to offering fuel-efficient choices across its lineup, aligning with shifting consumer preferences and regulatory pressures toward electrification.

The story

Ford Motor Company is preparing to introduce a new small SUV positioned at the budget-conscious end of the market, with plans to begin sales by 2029. According to Car and Driver, Ford displayed early mockups of the vehicle at a recent dealer event, revealing a model designed to compete aggressively in the entry-level segment with a starting price of $25,000. The automaker is planning to offer both hybrid and nonhybrid powertrain options, giving buyers flexibility in fuel efficiency and cost.

The announcement represents a significant strategic move for the Dearborn, Michigan-based manufacturer. Ford Motor Company, founded by Henry Ford in 1903, has long maintained a presence across multiple market segments through its Ford brand and its luxury Lincoln division. By introducing an affordable SUV option, the company aims to capture price-sensitive customers who have increasingly gravitated toward SUVs over traditional sedans in recent years.

The timing of this release aligns with Ford's broader strategy to modernize its vehicle lineup and respond to evolving consumer preferences. The inclusion of hybrid powertrains signals that the company is preparing its entry-level offerings to meet growing expectations around fuel economy without requiring customers to move to fully electric vehicles. This dual-powertrain approach allows Ford to appeal to both traditionally minded buyers and those seeking incremental steps toward greener transportation.

The $25,000 price point represents an aggressive positioning that could reshape the competitive landscape for affordable SUVs. At that price level, Ford would be targeting buyers currently considering vehicles from manufacturers known for value-oriented offerings. The early mockups shown at the dealer event suggest the vehicle is still in development, with the multi-year runway until 2029 providing Ford time to refine design, engineering, and manufacturing processes.

The project underscores Ford's commitment to maintaining relevance across all market tiers during a period of significant industry transformation. As automakers navigate electrification mandates and shifting consumer preferences, maintaining a robust entry-level offering has become increasingly important for market share and brand loyalty. Ford's strategy of offering multiple powertrain choices at the affordable end reflects the reality that consumer adoption of electric vehicles remains gradual, and hybrid technology serves as a transitional option for many buyers.
